# Reward, Desire, and the Wanting-Liking Divide: The Engagement-Loop Neuroscience — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **reward, Desire, and the Wanting-Liking Divide: The Engagement-Loop Neuroscience** within The subconscious-influence discipline of advertising — how sensory inputs, colors, emotional triggers, and design elements shape purchasing behavior before the consumer realizes it, spanning consumer neuroscience measurement, perception and attention science, multisensory marketing, affect and memory systems, and the ethics boundary separating legitimate subconscious design from neuromyth and manipulation..

Subject scope: Covers the motivation neuroscience whose reward circuits power desire, craving, and engagement-loop design — and whose exploitation risks define a central ethics frontier: the reward-system architecture (the mesolimbic-circuitry literacy where dopaminergic pathways from midbrain to striatum and prefrontal regions implement incentive salience, making reward-associated cues themselves motivating per the reward literatures of the brain section; the prediction-error-learning mechanism where outcomes versus expectation update cue value, the computational core of learned desire whose commercial expression includes surprise mechanics and expectation management per the reinforcement-learning traditions; the incentive-salience concept where cues acquire magnetic attention-grabbing and approach-triggering properties through association, explaining why packaging, notifications, and brand stimuli provoke desire independent of conscious wanting per the salience literatures); the wanting-liking dissociation (the Berridge-distinction principle where wanting — incentive motivation driving pursuit — and liking — hedonic pleasure in consumption — run on separable systems, producing the documented patterns of desire without enjoyment and enjoyment without desire per the affective-neuroscience traditions; the addiction-model-insight where compulsive pursuit despite negative consequences reflects wanting-system hijacking with liking decline, the pattern whose commercial mimicry defines the exploitation boundary per the addiction literatures; the consumption-design implication where satisfaction engineering and desire engineering are distinct crafts — liking governs retention and word of mouth while wanting governs pursuit and impulse — demanding both per the experience-design traditions); the commercial-application systems (the anticipation-and-teaser engineering where delayed reveal, countdown mechanics, and partial information exploit prediction and incentive-salience systems per the launch literatures connecting to the scarcity section of the companion course; the variable-reward mechanics where unpredictable rewards in loyalty programs, gamified apps, and promotional games produce the strongest engagement persistence per the schedule literatures; the collection-and-completion drives where set completion, progress mechanics, and achievement systems engage documented goal-gradient and completion motivation per the gamification literatures of the retention companion section; the craving-cycle design in food, beverage, and habit categories where sensory-specific satiety management, flavor architecture, and cue exposure structure repeat desire per the appetite literatures; the social-reward integration where likes, shares, and status recognition exploit social-evaluation reward circuitry per the social-media literatures); the notification-and-attention economy (the interruption-response dynamics where alerts trigger orienting responses whose suppression costs cognitive effort, making notification design an attention-capture system per the interruption literatures; the compulsion-loop patterns where checking behavior reinforced by intermittent social and content rewards builds automatic engagement per the habit-loop connection of the retention section; the design-responsibility debate where engagement optimization and wellbeing conflict drives platform regulation, design-ethics movements, and the attention-rights discussion per the tech-ethics literatures); the measurement discipline (the approach-motivation measurement where frontal-asymmetry, pupil dilation, reaction-time approach tendencies, and behavioral pursuit metrics index wanting with the tool-reliability caveats of the measurement section; the liking-assessment methods where consumption-phase facial response, self-report, and repeat-choice behavior measure enjoyment separately from pursuit per the dissociation-testing traditions; the loop-behavior-analytics where engagement patterns, session structure, and return dynamics reveal operative reward mechanics in production systems per the product-analytics literatures); and the exploitation-ethics frontier (the dark-engagement taxonomy where variable-reward harvesting, streak-anxiety mechanics, infinite-scroll absence of stopping cues, and regret-minimization traps constitute the manipulation patterns regulators and ethicists catalog per the dark-pattern literatures of the companion course; the vulnerability-dimension where developing brains, addiction histories, and impulse-control difficulties face heightened harm from reward-loop design per the vulnerability literatures; the sustainable-engagement principle where loops serving genuine value produce retention without resentment, while extraction loops accumulate churn, regulation, and reputational cost per the long-term-value traditions; the regulatory-horizon awareness where gambling-adjacency concerns in loot-box mechanics, minor-protection rules, and digital-wellbeing legislation reshape permissible design per the regulation-tracking disciplines) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the loop-extractor whose engagement mechanics pursue wanting-hijacking patterns that generate usage metrics alongside growing resentment and regulatory attention, remediated by the sustainable-engagement principle; the liking-neglector whose desire-engineered campaigns drive trial that consumption experience fails to retain, remediated by the dissociation literacy; the variable-reward-casino operator whose gambling-adjacent mechanics attract minor-protection and loot-box enforcement, remediated by the regulatory-horizon awareness; the notification-spammer whose interruption volume captures attention short-term while training systematic app dismissal, remediated by the interruption-response literacy; the unmeasured-craving-designer whose reward mechanics deploy without approach-motivation or loop analytics verification, remediated by the measurement discipline; and the autonomy-dismissing optimizer whose engagement science treats user wellbeing as externality until platform policy or press forces reckoning, remediated by the exploitation-ethics frontier with detection methods as the diagnostic.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

The module deepens this section's capabilities for a learner progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and connects them to the surrounding discipline rather than teaching them in isolation.

## Core Output Requirements

- Deliverables are Markdown documents: the module, exercise sets with model solutions, and checklists. No placeholders, no "TODO" sections.
- Ground content in authoritative sources:
- Phil Barden, Decoded: The Science Behind Why We Buy — the practitioner bridge applying dual-process decision science to marketing stimuli, value, and framing
- Patrick Renvoisé & Christophe Morin, Neuromarketing: Understanding the Buy Buttons in the Brain — applied stimulus design from contrast, tangibility, and emotion principles
- McClure, Li, Tomlin, Cypert, Montague & Montague (2004), Neural Correlates of Behavioral Preference for Culturally Familiar Drinks, Neuron — the canonical Pepsi-Paradox fMRI study anchoring brand-effect neuroscience
- Dan Hill, Emotionomics — the role of emotional states and facial-response measurement in advertising effectiveness
- Kenneth Holmqvist et al., Eye Tracking: A Comprehensive Guide to Methods and Measures — the methodological standard for attention measurement
- Binet & Field, The Long and the Short of It, with the IPA Effectiveness databank — evidence base for emotional-reach versus rational-persuasion advertising effects
- NMSBA (Neuromarketing Science and Business Association) — buying guides, code of conduct, and vendor standards for neuromarketing research practice
- Maintain an evidence ledger while writing: every factual claim or number is either sourced, flagged as disputed/popular account, or omitted. Never invent statistics, studies, or citations.
- Distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations, and say which is which.
- Explain each specialized term in clear language on first use.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Neuromyth laundering: dressing ordinary preference research in brain-imaging imagery and borrowed authority, buying credibility without adding measurement value; Vendor-report credulity: commissioning black-box studies whose methods, baselines, and sample sizes never face scrutiny, then defending unreplicable claims in strategy meetings; Subliminal hyperbole: believing mind-control myths about hidden messages while ignoring the real, bounded effects of supraliminal priming, salience, and fluency; Tool fascination: selecting fMRI or EEG for questions that eye tracking, implicit tests, or well-designed surveys answer better, faster, and cheaper; Scanner-to-market leap: treating neural activation in artificial lab conditions as purchase prediction without behavioral validation, effect-size realism, and field testing; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
