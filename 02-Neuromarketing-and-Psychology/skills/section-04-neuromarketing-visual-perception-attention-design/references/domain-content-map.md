## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the perception science whose pre-conscious visual processing determines whether any advertising stimulus gets seen, parsed, and evaluated at all: the visual-intake architecture (the early-filtering bottleneck where the visual system discards most scene information through rapid selection, giving the first hundred milliseconds of exposure disproportionate influence per the vision literatures; the two-stream organization where location-and-motion processing and object-and-recognition processing run parallel pathways whose design implications differ per the visual-neuroscience traditions; the pre-attentive-feature inventory where color, orientation, size, motion, and basic form register before conscious attention with the search-asymmetry evidence that popping-out features guide gaze automatically per the feature-integration literatures); the salience and competition mechanics (the bottom-up-salience concept where contrast, isolation, intensity, and novelty compute into attentional priority maps whose models predict first-fixation locations with documented accuracy per the salience literatures; the top-down modulation where task goals, expectations, and brand familiarity override raw salience, meaning the same design performs differently for searchers versus browsers per the attention-control traditions; the clutter-competition reality where salience operates relative to environment, demanding shelf, feed, and page context testing rather than isolated-design judgment per the context literatures; the banner-blindness and ad-avoidance patterns where learned irrelevance filters suppress processing of ad-positioned and ad-styled elements, favoring native integration and distinctive-but-relevant salience per the avoidance literatures); the Gestalt organization principles (the proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, and figure-ground laws where the visual system imposes structure automatically, determining whether layouts read as unified compositions or confusing fragments per the Gestalt traditions; the grouping-as-message principle where visual organization itself communicates hierarchy, relationship, and importance before any copy processes; the violation-effect craft where deliberate pattern breaks capture attention through incongruity whose resolution reward depends on comprehension feasibility per the incongruity literatures); the gaze-guidance toolkit (the face-and-gaze-cue processing where human faces attract fixation automatically and depicted gaze direction steers viewer attention toward adjacent elements, the documented cue-following effect per the social-vision literatures; the visual-pathway design where size gradients, line direction, and element sequencing construct intended scanpaths whose verification uses the eye-tracking methods of the prior section; the focal-hierarchy discipline where one dominant element anchors each composition because competing focal points divide the bottleneck-limited processing per the hierarchy traditions); the fluency and complexity balance (the processing-fluency connection where easily parsed visuals generate the preference, familiarity, and truth effects documented in the behavioral-economics companion, now grounded in perceptual mechanism; the complexity-optimum research where visual richness attracts in moderation while overload triggers avoidance, with the optimum shifting by category, audience expertise, and exposure context per the complexity literatures; the symmetry, prototype, and typicality effects where category-typical designs process fluently while moderate atypicality balances distinctiveness against comprehension per the aesthetic-preference literatures); the applied-perception domains (the package-perception science where shelf-distance viewing, color-block recognition, shape silhouette, and label hierarchy determine grab-and-parse success per the packaging literatures of the channels section; the digital-feed-perception reality where scroll velocity compresses exposure windows demanding instant figure-ground clarity and the brand-and-offer recognition speed that thumbnail-first design serves; the typography-and-legibility discipline where font weight, size, case, and contrast modulate both readability and tone perception per the typography literatures; the motion-and-video-attention dynamics where movement captures orienting responses while pacing determines sustained attention per the video literatures of the applied sections) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the isolated-design-judge whose beautiful creative fails because relative salience in actual environment context never got tested, remediated by the clutter-competition discipline; the multi-focal-chaos layout whose competing focal points guarantee that none receives bottleneck-limited processing, remediated by the hierarchy discipline; the ad-styled-native failure whose obviously-advertising appearance triggers learned avoidance filters, remediated by the banner-blindness literacy; the fluency-sacrificing-distinctivist whose radical design breaks comprehension while pursuing difference, remediated by the complexity-optimum balance; the gaze-cue-waster whose model looks at the camera while the offer sits unnoticed in the corner, remediated by the cue-following craft; and the unverified-hierarchy claimant whose scanpath assumptions never faced eye-tracking measurement despite accessible tools, remediated by the verification practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
