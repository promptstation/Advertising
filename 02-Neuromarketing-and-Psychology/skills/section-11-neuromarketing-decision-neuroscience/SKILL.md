# Decision Neuroscience: Valuation, Price Processing, and Choice in the Brain — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **decision Neuroscience: Valuation, Price Processing, and Choice in the Brain** within The subconscious-influence discipline of advertising — how sensory inputs, colors, emotional triggers, and design elements shape purchasing behavior before the consumer realizes it, spanning consumer neuroscience measurement, perception and attention science, multisensory marketing, affect and memory systems, and the ethics boundary separating legitimate subconscious design from neuromyth and manipulation..

Subject scope: Covers the neuroeconomics layer where valuation, pricing, and choice mechanics of the companion behavioral course receive their biological grounding and measurement access: the valuation-system literacy (the common-currency-integration where heterogeneous product attributes, prices, and emotional reactions converge into unified subjective-value signals whose neural correlates track revealed preference and willingness to pay per the neuroeconomics traditions of the brain section; the attribute-integration dynamics where value computation weights attributes non-linearly with reference dependence, matching the prospect-theory patterns at the neural level per the neuroeconomic-prospect literatures; the state-modulation principle where hunger, mood, arousal, and time pressure shift valuation weighting, explaining context effects on price sensitivity and impulse per the state literatures); the price-processing neuroscience (the pain-of-paying-neural evidence where excessive price presentation engages aversion-associated processing including the documented insula response patterns per the price-neuroscience literatures; the payment-form-modulation where decoupled payment methods reduce aversive response signatures, the neural correlate of the cash-versus-card effects in the pricing section of the companion course; the discount-and-gain processing where promotional frames engage reward-anticipation circuitry with the reference-price-dependence of whether identical prices read as gain or loss per the promotion literatures; the price-quality-inference circuitry where premium pricing activates expectation and experience-modulation pathways including the documented wine-taste studies where price altered experienced pleasantness and its neural signature per the marketing-placebo literatures); the choice-process neuroscience (the option-comparison dynamics where choice among alternatives engages valuation comparison with documented response-time and conflict signatures including the compromise-effect correlates per the choice literatures; the choice-overload-evidence where large option sets produce decision-difficulty signatures and deferral, the neural backing of the assortment research in the companion course; the default-processing-advantage where default options receive reduced deliberative processing and implied-endorsement valuation per the default literatures; the self-control-conflict architecture where impulse and deliberation systems compete in intertemporal and indulgence choices, the substrate of commitment-device and friction design per the dual-system traditions); the willingness-to-pay measurement practice (the neural-WTP-estimation methods where valuation-region activity predicts price acceptance with documented but modest incremental validity over behavioral measures per the WTP literatures; the bid-behavioral-methods where auction-style and choice-based incentive-compatible procedures remain the practical standard for price research per the conjoint traditions connecting to the analytics companion; the price-testing-integration where neural and physiological measures supplement rather than replace the experimental price testing of the companion discipline per the triangulation standards); the neuromarketing-of-decision applications (the offer-structure-optimization where bundle, tier, and guarantee designs face response measurement at valuation and aversion levels per the applied literatures; the shelf-and-menu architecture testing where arrangement effects on choice receive attention and preference measurement per the retail literatures of the applied section; the checkout-flow assessment where friction and payment moments face stress and abandonment-signal measurement per the UX literatures; the promotion-response prediction where deal framing effects get tested before mass deployment per the testing traditions); and the interpretation-humility discipline (the reverse-inference-hazard where observing region activation and concluding specific mental state commits the documented logical error because regions serve multiple functions, the most common vendor-report flaw per the neuroimaging-critique literatures; the small-effect-realism where neural decision findings carry effect sizes whose commercial translation requires field validation per the evidence-grading habits; the individual-variation-management where valuation circuitry varies across people making population-average claims weak for individual prediction per the variation literatures; the behavior-anchor principle where neural measures earn trust through demonstrated correlation with actual choice and purchase data per the validation traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the reverse-inference-committer whose reports read insula activity as proof of price pain without the logical and replication support, remediated by the interpretation-humility discipline; the neural-WTP-overtruster whose scanner-based price predictions skip incentive-compatible behavioral validation, remediated by the behavior-anchor principle; the state-blind-pricer whose price tests run on sated, rested, unpressured samples and generalize to hungry, tired, rushed shoppers, remediated by the state-modulation literacy; the discount-circuit-chaser whose promotion design maximizes reward-anticipation activation while ignoring the reference-price erosion documented in the companion course, remediated by the integration discipline; the choice-overload-denier whose mega-assortment strategy treats decision-difficulty signatures as noise, remediated by the choice-process literacy; and the vendor-neuro-spectacle buyer whose decision studies pay for imaging glamour that conjoint methods answered cheaply, remediated by the triangulation standards with detection methods as the diagnostic.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

The module deepens this section's capabilities for a learner progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and connects them to the surrounding discipline rather than teaching them in isolation.

- Ground content in authoritative sources:
- Phil Barden, Decoded: The Science Behind Why We Buy — the practitioner bridge applying dual-process decision science to marketing stimuli, value, and framing
- Patrick Renvoisé & Christophe Morin, Neuromarketing: Understanding the Buy Buttons in the Brain — applied stimulus design from contrast, tangibility, and emotion principles

- Kenneth Holmqvist et al., Eye Tracking: A Comprehensive Guide to Methods and Measures — the methodological standard for attention measurement

- Maintain an evidence ledger while writing: every factual claim or number is either sourced, flagged as disputed/popular account, or omitted. Never invent statistics, studies, or citations.

- Explain each specialized term in clear language on first use.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Neuromyth laundering: dressing ordinary preference research in brain-imaging imagery and borrowed authority, buying credibility without adding measurement value; Vendor-report credulity: commissioning black-box studies whose methods, baselines, and sample sizes never face scrutiny, then defending unreplicable claims in strategy meetings; Subliminal hyperbole: believing mind-control myths about hidden messages while ignoring the real, bounded effects of supraliminal priming, salience, and fluency; Tool fascination: selecting fMRI or EEG for questions that eye tracking, implicit tests, or well-designed surveys answer better, faster, and cheaper; Scanner-to-market leap: treating neural activation in artificial lab conditions as purchase prediction without behavioral validation, effect-size realism, and field testing; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
