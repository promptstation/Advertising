## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Extends sensory influence beyond the visual into the modalities whose subconscious effects on evaluation and purchase the discipline documents: the crossmodal foundation (the sensory-integration principle where the brain combines modality signals into unified product perception, making taste partly visual, weight partly haptic, and flavor partly auditory per the multisensory literatures; the crossmodal-correspondence catalog where documented pairings including high pitch with lightness and brightness, low pitch with depth and richness, round shapes with sweetness, and angular shapes with bitterness structure legitimate sensory design per the correspondence research; the congruence-effect discipline where matched sensory cues enhance evaluation and processing fluency while mismatched cues either disrupt or, in deliberate incongruity strategy, capture attention per the congruence literatures; the sensory-dominance hierarchy where vision typically dominates but olfaction and taste carry privileged emotional-memory access per the modality literatures); the auditory system (the sonic-branding architecture where audio logos, brand melodies, and sonic palettes build recognition and association through repeated pairing per the sonic-identity traditions; the music-effect research where tempo, mode, volume, and genre influence dwell time, perceived waiting duration, arousal, and even purchase behavior including the documented wine-selection effects of played music per the music-marketing literatures; the voice-and-sound-symbolism findings where phoneme choices and voice qualities carry size, speed, and weight associations shaping naming and voiceover design per the sound-symbolism literatures; the silence-and-sound-scape craft where auditory absence and ambient layering shape premium perception and attention in retail and media contexts per the soundscape literatures); the olfactory system (the scent-memory-privilege principle where olfactory processing connects directly to emotional-memory circuitry giving scents unusual association-strength and recall-triggering power per the olfaction literatures; the ambient-scenting evidence where congruent environmental scents measurably influence dwell time, evaluation, and purchase in retail and hospitality contexts with effect conditions documented per the scent-marketing research; the product-scent-design where fragrance in and on products carries quality, freshness, and category signals including the scent-transfer effects where ambient smell colors product judgment per the sensory-product literatures; the ethics-and-sensitivity discipline where scent deployment faces allergy, consent, and manipulation concerns because olfaction cannot be visually avoided per the sensory-ethics literatures); the haptic and gustatory systems (the touch-ownership-effect research where physical handling increases valuation and endowment, the mechanism behind test-drive, sample, and interactive-display strategy per the haptic literatures; the weight-and-material-signaling where heft communicates quality and material texture communicates category positioning per the product-semantics literatures; the digital-haptic-substitutes where animation, micro-interaction feedback, and material-suggestive visuals partially compensate absent touch per the interface literatures; the taste-interaction findings where expectation from packaging, color, and sound measurably alters experienced flavor per the gastrophysics traditions); the sensory-orchestration practice (the brand-sensory-audit methodology where existing touchpoints get inventoried across modalities revealing gaps, conflicts, and opportunities per the sensory-strategy traditions; the congruence-design discipline where selected sensory cues align with brand positioning, category expectation, and each other rather than accumulating independently; the modality-budget judgment where investment follows audience-context fit — scent suits physical retail, sound suits video and radio, haptics suit packaging — per the channel literatures of the applied section; the measurement-integration where biometric and behavioral methods assess sensory interventions per the toolkit discipline) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the visual-only-sensory strategist whose brand experience ignores the documented crossmodal majority of product perception, remediated by the integration principle; the incongruence-accumulator whose store scent contradicts the playlist contradicts the packaging, fragmenting perception across modalities, remediated by the congruence discipline; the scent-blaster whose ambient fragrance ignores sensitivity, consent, and saturation effects until complaints arrive, remediated by the olfactory-ethics discipline; the music-as-filler operator whose retail and media audio runs unselected despite tempo and genre effects on behavior, remediated by the music-effect research; the haptic-neglecting-packager whose unboxing experience forfeits the touch-ownership valuation boost, remediated by the haptic literacies; and the crossmodal-literalist whose correspondence-chart application ignores category convention and audience learning, remediated by the congruence-context discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
