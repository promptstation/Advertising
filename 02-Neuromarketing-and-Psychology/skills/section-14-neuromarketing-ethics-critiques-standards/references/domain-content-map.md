## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the critical and ethical framework whose mastery separates professional neuromarketing from neuromyth commerce, protecting clients, consumers, and the field's legitimacy: the scientific-critique literacy (the reverse-inference-problem documentation where brain-region activation cannot logically establish specific mental states because regions serve multiple functions, the foundational critique of neuro-overclaiming per the neuroimaging-critique literatures; the neuro-realism-effect research where brain imagery and vocabulary lend unearned credibility to otherwise ordinary claims, making practitioners complicit in deception unless disciplined per the neuro-seduction literatures; the incremental-validity question where neural measures must demonstrate prediction beyond cheaper behavioral and self-report methods to justify cost, a burden much vendor marketing never meets per the validation literatures; the methodological-limitations inventory where laboratory artificiality, sample constraints, signal noise, and analysis flexibility bound all findings per the research-practice section); the field-integrity standards (the professional-body frameworks including NMSBA code-of-conduct provisions on methodological transparency, claim substantiation, and participant welfare per the association standards; the publication-and-disclosure norms where methods, limitations, and conflicts of interest accompany findings per the open-science traditions; the practitioner-claim-discipline where statements about what studies show stay within what designs support, distinguishing demonstrated effects, plausible mechanisms, and speculation in all client communication per the honest-capability traditions of the priming section; the vendor-accountability practice where the buying-side methodology interrogation of the research section functions as market discipline against black-box claims); the consumer-ethics framework (the autonomy-and-awareness principle where subconscious-targeted influence raises distinct consent questions because influence undetectable cannot be resisted through scrutiny, per the manipulation literatures of the companion course; the vulnerability-protection obligations where children, cognitively impaired, financially distressed, and addiction-susceptible populations face heightened harm from reward-loop and craving design per the vulnerability literatures of the motivation section; the privacy-dimension where physiological and neural data constitute uniquely intimate information whose collection, storage, and inference use demand the highest consent and security standards per the neuro-privacy discussions; the manipulation-spectrum application where sensory and emotional design ranges from fluent communication through persuasion to exploitation by the same transparency, benefit, and reversibility tests of the companion ethics framework); the regulatory-and-platform landscape (the advertising-standards coverage where deception rules, health-claim substantiation, and children's-advertising codes constrain sensory and emotional techniques regardless of their subconscious operation per the advertising-law traditions; the digital-platform policies where app-store, social-platform, and ad-network rules increasingly address dark engagement patterns and targeting sensitivity per the platform-governance literatures; the neurotechnology-regulation-horizon where emerging neurorights discussions and data-protection extensions anticipate consumer neural-data use per the frontier literatures; the cross-jurisdiction variation where European, American, and Asian regulatory traditions differ on implicit-targeting and child-protection strictness per the international-compliance traditions); the professional-practice integration (the ethics-review-workflow where campaign and study proposals face structured review of influence techniques, audience vulnerability, data handling, and claim honesty before deployment per the governance traditions of the companion course; the dissent-and-escalation channels where team members can flag concerns without career cost, the culture condition of ethical practice per the organizational-ethics literatures; the consumer-respect-positioning where the discipline frames its purpose as reducing friction, improving fit, and communicating fluently — making better products win — rather than overriding judgment per the professional-identity traditions; the field-stewardship orientation where practitioners correct neuromyths publicly, share validation findings, and refuse myth-selling engagements because field credibility is a shared asset per the stewardship traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the neuro-seducer whose decks deploy brain imagery to manufacture credibility for ordinary claims, exploiting the documented realism effect, remediated by the claim discipline; the validity-evader whose vendor relationships never demand incremental-validity evidence because interrogation threatens the arrangement, remediated by the accountability practice; the autonomy-trivializer who treats all influence-resistance concerns as marketing romanticism despite the genuine asymmetry of undetectable techniques, remediated by the consumer-ethics framework; the vulnerability-blind-deployer whose craving and reward mechanics reach children and susceptible adults because targeting convenience ignored harm asymmetry, remediated by the protection obligations; the privacy-careless-collector whose biometric data handling follows ordinary market-research practice despite neural and physiological intimacy, remediated by the privacy dimension; and the myth-market-participant whose revenue depends on subliminal and buy-button narratives, financing the field's credibility destruction, remediated by the stewardship orientation with detection methods as the diagnostic.
