# Part A: Practical Exercises

## Exercise 1 — Keep the discipline honest through critique literacy (reverse-inference problems
**Scenario:** provide a realistic Neuromarketing & Psychology scenario appropriate to the audience (real or closely modeled on documented practice).
**Objective:** keep the discipline honest through critique literacy (reverse-inference problems, neuro-realism effects, incremental-validity questions, limitation inventories), integrity standards (professional frameworks, disclosure norms, claim discipline, vendor accountability), consumer ethics (autonomy principles, vulnerability protection, privacy dimensions, manipulation spectrums), the regulatory landscape (advertising standards, platform policies, neurorights horizon, jurisdiction variation), and practice integration (ethics review, dissent channels, respect positioning, field stewardship), avoiding neuro-seduction, validity evasion, autonomy trivializing, vulnerability blindness, privacy carelessness, and myth-market participation failures.
**Constraints:** must use the framework taught in the corresponding unit; must state assumptions and trade-offs; one deliverable, length-bounded.
**Deliverable:** the professional artifact this capability produces (memo, table, plan, analysis, or model — as applicable).
**Evaluation criteria:** correct application of the framework; decisions justified with evidence; limitations honestly stated; artifact usable by a colleague without further explanation.

## Exercise 2 — Integrated capstone
**Scenario:** one realistic problem spanning the whole section.
**Objective:** take the problem through every unit's framework in sequence.
**Constraints:** all unit artifacts required and internally consistent; evidence discipline maintained throughout.
**Deliverable:** a coherent campaign/portfolio document assembled as real professional documentation.
**Evaluation criteria:** consistency across artifacts; each capability demonstrably exercised; trade-offs stated at each decision point.

# Part B: Professional Gate Checklists

Use verbatim as module appendices and as workshop job aids. Every item must
force a decision or produce an artifact — items that are merely
inspirational get cut.

## B1. Keep the discipline honest checklist
- [ ] Keep the discipline honest through critique literacy (reverse-inference problems, neuro-realism effects, incremental-validity questions, limitation inventories), integrity standards (professional frameworks, disclosure norms, claim discipline, vendor accountability), consumer ethics (autonomy principles, vulnerability protection, privacy dimensions, manipulation spectrums), the regulatory landscape (advertising standards, platform policies, neurorights horizon, jurisdiction variation), and practice integration (ethics review, dissent channels, respect positioning, field stewardship), avoiding neuro-seduction, validity evasion, autonomy trivializing, vulnerability blindness, privacy carelessness, and myth-market participation failures?
- [ ] Decisions and their justification are documented for review by a colleague

## B2. Delivery quality checklist
- [ ] Every claim sourced, graded, or flagged as contested?
- [ ] All terminology explained on first use?
- [ ] Trade-offs stated wherever recommendations are context-dependent?
- [ ] Material reads as practitioner-written, not generic?
