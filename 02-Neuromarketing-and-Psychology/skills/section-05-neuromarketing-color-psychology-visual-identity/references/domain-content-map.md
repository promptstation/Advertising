## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the color discipline whose psychological effects on emotion, perception, and behavior the course description names explicitly, separated into what evidence supports and what marketing folklore asserts: the color-perception foundation (the physiology-and-culture layering where wavelength processing is universal but color association is substantially learned through cultural exposure, category convention, and personal experience per the color literatures; the context-dependence principle where identical hues read differently against backgrounds, adjacent colors, lighting, and material finishes, making swatch-level claims unreliable per the color-context research; the arousal-and-valence evidence where saturated warm hues carry documented activation effects and cool desaturated tones carry calming associations with effect sizes modest and context-bound per the empirical literatures; the cross-cultural-variation catalog where white associates with purity and mourning across different cultures and red spans luck, danger, and appetite by region, demanding audience-specific validation per the cross-cultural literatures of the anthropology companion); the association-formation mechanics (the category-convention system where color meanings consolidate within product categories — blue in technology and finance, green in natural and health positioning, black in luxury — creating fluency advantages for convention-following and distinctiveness advantages for strategic deviation per the category-color literatures; the brand-color-equity process where sustained pairing transfers color ownership in consumer memory, the mechanism behind signature brand palettes whose protection extends to trademark in documented cases per the brand-identity traditions; the learned-appetite-and-quality associations where food coloring conventions shape freshness, flavor-intensity, and quality inference per the food-psychology literatures; the price-and-quality-signaling research where palette choices including minimalism, metallics, and saturation levels carry premium versus value readings per the signaling literatures connecting to the pricing psychology of the companion course); the applied-color decision system (the brand-palette architecture where primary, secondary, and accent colors organize recognition, hierarchy, and campaign flexibility per the identity-system traditions; the conversion-element evidence audit where button and call-to-action color testing produces context-specific winners whose isolation effects — contrast against page, not hue magic — explain most results per the testing literatures of the experimentation companion; the packaging-color strategy where shelf-context visibility, category signaling, and variant differentiation combine per the packaging literatures; the environmental-color applications where retail and space coloring influences dwell time and perception with the evidence-caution that laboratory color-mood findings transfer weakly to commercial settings per the environmental-psychology literatures); the measurement-and-validation discipline (the association-testing methods where implicit and explicit measures map actual audience color associations rather than assumed ones per the measurement toolkit of the prior sections; the eye-tracking-color interaction where color affects salience and scan patterns measurably per the attention literatures; the A/B-validation standard where color decisions with conversion stakes face the experimentation discipline rather than palette-preference debate per the testing traditions; the accessibility-compliance requirements where contrast ratios serve readability standards and visually impaired users, a legal and ethical floor beneath aesthetic preference per the accessibility standards); and the folklore-correction discipline (the universal-meaning myth audit where popular color-psychology charts asserting fixed emotional effects face the context and culture evidence, requiring practitioner skepticism per the neuromyth-correction traditions of the foundations section; the single-study-overgeneralization guard where isolated findings including celebrated color-performance claims receive replication scrutiny before budget influence; the preference-versus-performance distinction where stakeholder color liking predicts nothing about audience response, demanding audience measurement over conference-room taste per the research literatures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the chart-psychologist whose palette decisions cite universal color-meaning tables ignoring culture, category, and context, remediated by the association-formation literacy; the button-color-obsessor whose conversion program rotates call-to-action hues while offer, proof, and hierarchy go untested, remediated by the isolation-effect literacy; the swatch-decider whose brand colors get selected from isolated samples rather than in-context application across packaging, screens, and environments, remediated by the context-dependence principle; the convention-blind-distinctivist whose category-defying palette sacrifices recognition fluency for difference without strategic rationale, remediated by the convention-system literacy; the accessibility-afterthought designer whose low-contrast palettes exclude impaired users and fail compliance review, remediated by the accessibility requirements; and the unvalidated-assumption importer whose cross-cultural campaigns carry home-market color meanings into markets where they invert, remediated by the cross-cultural validation with detection methods as the diagnostic.
