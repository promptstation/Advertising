Understand the consumer brain through processing pathways (sensory intake, dual-process hardware, embodiment), the attention system (orienting response, salience competition, resource limits), the emotion system (appraisal and arousal, threat processing, valence mapping with discrete nuances), the memory system (encoding dependency, associative networks, consolidation dynamics), the reward system (mesolimbic circuitry, common-currency valuation, prediction-error learning), the aversion system (loss and price pain, disgust sensitivity), and systems integration (parallel operation, individual differences, state dependence), avoiding single-region attribution, attention assumptions, emotion flattening, memory neglect, and state blindness failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Maps the neural and physiological systems whose operation the practitioner must understand to interpret any measurement and design any stimulus: the processing-pathway overview (the sensory-intake architecture where visual, auditory, olfactory, and somatosensory streams undergo early filtering before conscious registration, the bottleneck that makes pre-attentive design powerful per the perception literatures; the dual-process neural picture where fast evaluative circuits and slower deliberative networks implement the System 1 and System 2 distinction of the behavioral-economics companion in biological hardware; the embodiment principle where bodily states including heartbeat, posture, and facial configuration feed back into evaluation per the interoception and somatic-marker traditions); the attention system (the orienting response where novel, moving, loud, or emotionally charged stimuli capture processing automatically, the reflexive gateway of all advertising exposure per the attention literatures; the salience-network operation where bottom-up stimulus features compete against top-down task goals for processing priority, determining whether an ad gets noticed in cluttered environments; the attention-resource reality where processing capacity limits mean most exposed stimuli receive shallow evaluation, favoring fluency-optimized design per the cognitive-load traditions); the emotion system (the appraisal-and-arousal architecture where stimuli undergo fast relevance and valence evaluation generating physiological response patterns including skin conductance, heart-rate change, and facial-muscle activation per the emotion literatures; the amygdala-and-threat-processing line where danger-relevant stimuli receive prioritized processing, the mechanism behind attention-grabbing fear appeals and their wear-out dynamics; the valence-approach-withdrawal mapping where positive affect motivates approach behavior and negative affect motivates withdrawal, with the nuance that specific negative emotions including sadness and guilt can motivate helping and purchase behavior per the discrete-emotion literatures of the emotion section); the memory system (the encoding-dependency principle where attention depth and emotional arousal determine whether stimuli enter memory at all per the encoding literatures; the associative-network model where brands exist as nodes connected to attributes, feelings, and usage contexts, with advertising building and strengthening the connections that determine mental availability per the brand-memory traditions; the consolidation-and-reconsolidation dynamics where emotional experiences consolidate preferentially and retrieved memories update, giving peak-moment design its durable influence per the memory literatures connecting to the peak-end findings of the companion course); the reward and valuation system (the mesolimbic-reward circuitry where anticipation of gain releases dopaminergic activity driving approach motivation, the neural substrate of desire, craving, and the wanting-versus-liking distinction per the reward literatures; the common-currency valuation where diverse goods and offers get integrated into a single subjective-value signal whose neural correlates track willingness to pay per the neuroeconomics traditions; the prediction-error learning where outcomes better or worse than expected update stimulus value, the mechanism behind variable rewards, surprise-and-delight tactics, and promotion expectation training per the reinforcement traditions); the aversion and cost system (the loss-and-pain processing where price presentation and loss frames engage aversive response patterns including the documented insula activation to excessive prices per the neuroeconomics literatures; the disgust-and-contamination sensitivity where visual and conceptual impurity cues trigger powerful product rejection relevant to food, hygiene, and moral-positioning contexts per the disgust literatures); and the systems-integration view (the simultaneous-operation reality where attention, emotion, memory, reward, and aversion systems process every stimulus in parallel, making single-metric interpretations naive per the multi-system traditions; the individual-difference modulation where age, culture, expertise, and motivation shift system balance per the moderator literatures; the state-dependence principle where hunger, fatigue, mood, and context alter processing, demanding measurement-condition control per the research-practice section) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the single-region-attributor whose vendor reports claim the buy button lit up, ignoring that no purchase decision lives in one region and popular neuro vocabulary routinely misreads circuitry, remediated by the systems-integration view; the attention-assumer whose designs presume processing that the bottleneck and salience competition deny, remediated by the orienting-response literacy; the emotion-flattener whose briefs treat feeling as one dial instead of discrete systems with different motivations, remediated by the discrete-emotion architecture; the memory-neglecting activation-chaser whose campaigns win attention metrics while encoding nothing distinctive, remediated by the associative-network discipline; and the state-blind researcher whose lab conclusions ignore hunger, mood, and context moderators, remediated by the state-dependence principle with detection methods as the diagnostic.
