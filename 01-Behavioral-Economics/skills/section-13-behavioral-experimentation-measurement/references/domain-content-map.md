## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the experimentation discipline that converts behavioral hypotheses into verified campaign knowledge, the quality gate separating evidence-based practice from tactic folklore: the hypothesis-construction foundation (the mechanism-based-hypothesis format where predictions derive from identified bias, heuristic, or principle operating in a specified context rather than from tactic curiosity, giving failed tests diagnostic value per the theory-driven-testing traditions; the minimal-detectable-effect discipline where published behavioral effect sizes inform realistic expectations and sample-size requirements before launch per the power-analysis literatures; the one-mechanism-per-test principle where clean attribution requires isolating levers, with the factorial exception where combination effects are the explicit question); the testing-methodology toolkit (the A/B-testing infrastructure where randomized traffic splits compare control and variant with the platform mechanics of split allocation, exposure logging, and significance calculation per the experimentation literatures; the sample-size and duration calculation where power, baseline conversion, and detectable effect determine required traffic and calendar time including weekly seasonality coverage per the experimental-design traditions; the multivariate-testing extension where multiple elements combine efficiently with the interaction-interpretation demands; the sequential-testing awareness where peeking at results and stopping at significance inflates false-positive rates unless corrected methods apply per the sequential-analysis literatures; the quasi-experimental methods including geo-tests, switchbacks, and interrupted time series for contexts where user-level randomization fails per the field-experiment traditions); the behavioral-metrics architecture (the primary-conversion-metrics discipline where each test carries one decision metric preventing multiple-comparisons inflation per the metric-governance traditions; the mechanism-trace metrics where intermediate behaviors — time on offer, proof-section engagement, option-comparison patterns, scroll depth — verify that the hypothesized mechanism actually operated rather than conversion moving for unrelated reasons per the process-measurement literatures; the guardrail-metrics system where refund rates, complaint volumes, unsubscribe rates, support contacts, and brand-tracking scores bound the acceptable cost of conversion gains, the structural defense against dark-pattern drift per the guardrail traditions; the segment-heterogeneity analysis where behavioral effects vary by the decision-style, involvement, and lifecycle segments of the segmentation section, with the multiple-comparison caution on subgroup mining); the incrementality-and-value discipline (the uplift-measurement concept where the causal question is conversion change versus no-advertising baseline rather than last-click attribution per the incrementality literatures connecting to the media-mathematics companion course; the long-short-tension measurement where behavioral conversion lifts face comparison against brand-health and customer-lifetime-value effects per the effectiveness-research traditions including the IPA databank and Binet-Field findings on emotional-reach versus rational-conversion balance; the winner's-curse correction where variant effects measured in selection shrink on retest, demanding replication before scaling per the replication literatures; the revenue-and-margin translation where conversion-point gains convert through average order value, retention effects, and cost structure into business impact per the measurement-to-money traditions); the experimentation-program operations (the test-portfolio-management where hypothesis backlogs prioritize by mechanism importance, traffic feasibility, and expected value per the experimentation-program literatures; the learning-documentation systems where every test — especially failures — accumulates into institutional knowledge bases preventing repetition and building mechanism understanding per the knowledge-management traditions; the velocity-versus-rigor balance where testing throughput maintains statistical and operational standards including proper randomization, exposure verification, and no-launch-without-significance discipline; the platform-and-tooling literacy where testing capabilities across web, app, email, and paid platforms differ in randomization quality and metric access per the martech literatures); and the statistical-sophistication requirements (the significance-and-confidence literacy where p-values and intervals get interpreted as evidence strength rather than binary truth per the statistical-reasoning traditions; the Simpson's-paradox and confound awareness where segment mixes shift between variants corrupting aggregate readings per the causal-inference literatures; the novelty-and-primacy-effect detection where behavioral changes from test exposure itself decay over time requiring extended runs per the novelty literatures; the Bayesian-alternative literacy where probability-of-better and expected-loss framings suit business decision contexts per the Bayesian-testing traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the tactic-fisherman whose tests ask does this trick work without mechanism hypotheses, producing results that neither replicate nor teach, remediated by the hypothesis-construction discipline; the peek-stopper whose daily significance checks halt tests at random highs, filling the learning base with false winners, remediated by the sequential-testing literacy; the metric-monoculturist whose tests judge on conversion alone while guardrails decay invisibly into refunds and churn, remediated by the metrics architecture; the underpowered-guesser whose tiny samples declare winners from noise, remediated by the sample-size discipline; the single-test-scaler whose unreplicated winner absorbs full budget before the winner's-curse shrinkage appears, remediated by the replication requirement; and the learning-loss team whose years of tests live in forgotten decks because documentation systems never existed, remediated by the program-operations discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
