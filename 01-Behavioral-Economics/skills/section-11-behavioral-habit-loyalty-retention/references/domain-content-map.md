## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the habit and loyalty discipline whose conversion of deliberate choice into automatic behavior produces the retention economics that sustain advertising investments: the habit-science foundation (the cue-routine-reward loop where context triggers, behavioral sequences, and reinforcing outcomes install automaticity through repetition per the habit-formation literatures; the automaticity measurement reality where behavior shifts from goal-directed to stimulus-controlled with documented implications for messaging — habits respond to context cues rather than arguments per the dual-system habit research; the variable-reward acceleration where unpredictable reinforcement schedules produce the strongest behavioral persistence per the operant-conditioning traditions whose commercial application carries the gambling-adjacency concerns of the ethics section; the investment-deepening principle where stored value, learned interfaces, and accumulated history raise switching resistance per the hook-model traditions); the loyalty-program behavioral engineering (the goal-gradient installation where progress-toward-reward structures accelerate purchase frequency with the endowed-progress boost per the gradient literatures of the journey section; the points-psychology system where earned currency creates mental-accounting dynamics — points feel like house money — inflating redemption engagement beyond cash-equivalent value per the currency literatures; the tier-and-status architecture where earned levels trigger status-loss aversion at renewal boundaries, the retention mechanism whose power scales with visible privilege differentiation per the status literatures; the program-value-honesty discipline where reward economics must survive customer arithmetic because devalued points and unreachable tiers convert loyalty programs into resentment generators per the program-failure case literatures); the subscription-behavior dynamics (the default-inertia engine where auto-renewal exploits status-quo bias at scale, the mechanism whose consumer-protection response including negative-option rules and click-to-cancel requirements defines the current compliance landscape per the enforcement frameworks; the payment-decoupling retention effect where billing invisibility suppresses per-period reevaluation, sustaining subscriptions through attention absence per the decoupling literatures; the cancellation-psychology craft where retention flows legitimately deploy win-back offers, pause options, and value reminders versus illegitimately deploying obstruction and confirmshaming per the dark-pattern taxonomy of the ethics section; the usage-engagement reality where payment inertia retains revenue only until accumulated non-use triggers eventual cancellation or chargeback, making engagement engineering the durable retention strategy per the subscription-economics literatures); the switching-cost taxonomy and management (the financial switching costs including lost points, remaining balances, and contract penalties whose transparency requirements face regulation; the procedural costs including setup effort, data migration, and relearning whose reduction or inflation defines ethical positioning per the sludge literatures; the relational and identity costs including community belonging and self-concept investment that unity-focused brand building deliberately cultivates per the identity literatures; the switching-window strategy where competitor failures, price changes, and life transitions open evaluation windows that behavioral campaigns target with proof, reciprocity, and risk-reversal per the disruption literatures); the retention-economics integration (the retention-margin arithmetic where percentage-point retention improvement compounds into customer-lifetime-value gains per the retention-economics literatures that make behavioral retention a financial discipline; the churn-prediction connection where behavioral signals including engagement decay and support contacts feed the predictive-analytics systems of the companion data course; the win-back-behavioral-design where lapsed-customer campaigns apply loss-framed status expiration, nostalgic familiarity, and updated-value proof per the reactivation literatures; the habit-brand-positioning where products embedding into routines — morning, commute, workday — achieve category-level automaticity per the routine-integration traditions); and the ethics-and-sustainability discipline (the exploitation-versus-value line where habit mechanics serve products genuinely worth repeating versus trapping consumers in unwanted continuation, the distinction enforcement and review culture increasingly police per the vulnerability literatures; the long-term-trust accounting where retention tactics that degrade trust destroy the brand asset funding them per the trust-economics traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the points-inflator whose loyalty currency devalues until program members feel cheated and churn with grievance, remediated by the value-honesty discipline; the obstruction-retainer whose cancellation mazes draw regulatory action and review-site exposure, remediated by the compliance and dark-pattern literacies; the inertia-only subscriber who retains wallets while engagement decays invisibly until mass lapse, remediated by the usage-engagement discipline; the message-to-habit mismatcher whose re-engagement campaigns argue benefits to consumers operating on context cues, remediated by the automaticity literacy; and the window-blind defender whose retention spend ignores predictable switching windows where competitor campaigns land unopposed, remediated by the window strategy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
