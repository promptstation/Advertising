## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Establishes the scientific base the entire course builds on, tracing how economics abandoned the rational-agent model and what that abandonment means for anyone designing campaigns: the classical model and its failure (the utility-maximizing consumer of textbook economics who holds stable preferences, processes all available information, weighs probabilities correctly, and chooses optimally, versus the observed reality of impulse purchases, brand switching under irrelevant cues, and systematic prediction failures that advertising practitioners encounter daily; the documented anomalies including preference reversals, framing effects on identical choices, and the gap between stated intentions and revealed purchases that no rational model accommodates); the founding intellectual line (Herbert Simon's bounded rationality, where cognitive limits, information limits, and time limits force satisficing rather than optimizing, establishing that decision quality is constrained by machinery rather than motivation; the Kahneman and Tversky heuristics-and-biases research program, where controlled experiments demonstrated systematic, predictable, replicable departures from rational judgment across representative populations; Richard Thaler's synthesis work including mental accounting, the endowment effect, and self-control problems, which assembled the anomalies into a coherent alternative economics earning the field its Nobel recognition; the consumption-specific extensions where consumer psychology researchers including Bettman, Payne, and Huber mapped decision strategies onto purchase contexts per the consumer decision research traditions); the dual-process architecture (the System 1 and System 2 framework where fast, automatic, associative, effortless processing handles most daily decisions while slow, deliberate, effortful processing engages only when triggered, with the advertiser's core insight that nearly all purchase decisions, brand evaluations, and ad responses run through System 1; the cognitive-miser principle where the brain rations attention and effort as scarce resources, making ease of processing a persuasive advantage in itself; the arousal and involvement modulation where high-stakes purchases recruit more System 2 scrutiny while habitual low-stakes purchases proceed almost entirely on autopilot per the involvement literatures); why this matters commercially (the campaign-design implication where messages, offers, and journeys engineered for automatic processing outperform those demanding deliberation; the prediction implication where behavioral regularities enable forecasting of consumer response better than assumption-based rational models; the competitive implication where firms that understand decision machinery convert demand rivals leave on the table; the measurement implication where behavioral theories generate testable hypotheses for experimentation programs rather than untestable claims); the replication-and-revision landscape (the post-2011 replication crisis where some celebrated effects including ego depletion and certain priming results failed to reproduce, demanding that practitioners distinguish robust findings from fragile ones through effect-size scrutiny and meta-analytic evidence per the open-science literatures; the robustness hierarchy where prospect theory findings, social proof effects, default power, and anchoring carry decades of replication while subtler cues require field validation before budget commitment; the preregistration and meta-analysis tools that let a working advertiser assess which behavioral science to trust); and the practitioner's translation discipline (the mechanism-first habit where every technique traces to an underlying cognitive mechanism rather than cargo-culted tactic lists; the context-qualification habit where boundary conditions including category, culture, price point, and purchase frequency accompany every principle; the evidence-grading habit where lab demonstrations, field experiments, and platform data receive differentiated trust weights per the scientific-literacy traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the rational-agent holdover whose campaign briefs still assume consumers read, compare, and optimize, producing information-dense ads nobody processes, remediated by the dual-process foundation; the jargon-fluent amateur who recites bias names in strategy meetings without designing a single intervention, remediated by the translation discipline; the pop-science importer who budgets around single-study effects that the replication literature has weakened or buried, remediated by the evidence-grading habit; the System-2-chaser whose clever copy demands deliberation from shoppers deciding in seconds, remediated by the cognitive-miser principle; and the anecdote-elevation practitioner whose one successful campaign becomes universal doctrine without mechanism or replication, remediated by the mechanism-first habit with detection methods as the diagnostic.
