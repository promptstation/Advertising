# Part A: Practical Exercises

## Exercise 1 — Ground advertising practice in behavioral science foundations through the classical model and its documented failures
**Scenario:** provide a realistic Behavioral Economics scenario appropriate to the audience (real or closely modeled on documented practice).
**Objective:** ground advertising practice in behavioral science foundations through the classical model and its documented failures, the founding line (bounded rationality, heuristics and biases, Thaler's synthesis, consumer-decision extensions), the dual-process architecture (System 1 and System 2, cognitive miser, involvement modulation), commercial relevance (design, prediction, competition, measurement implications), the replication landscape (crisis literacy, robustness hierarchy, meta-analytic tools), and practitioner translation discipline (mechanism-first, context-qualification, evidence-grading habits), avoiding rational-agent assumptions, jargon fluency without application, pop-science importation, deliberation-demanding creative, and anecdote elevation failures.
**Constraints:** must use the framework taught in the corresponding unit; must state assumptions and trade-offs; one deliverable, length-bounded.
**Deliverable:** the professional artifact this capability produces (memo, table, plan, analysis, or model — as applicable).
**Evaluation criteria:** correct application of the framework; decisions justified with evidence; limitations honestly stated; artifact usable by a colleague without further explanation.

## Exercise 2 — Integrated capstone
**Scenario:** one realistic problem spanning the whole section.
**Objective:** take the problem through every unit's framework in sequence.
**Constraints:** all unit artifacts required and internally consistent; evidence discipline maintained throughout.
**Deliverable:** a coherent campaign/portfolio document assembled as real professional documentation.
**Evaluation criteria:** consistency across artifacts; each capability demonstrably exercised; trade-offs stated at each decision point.
