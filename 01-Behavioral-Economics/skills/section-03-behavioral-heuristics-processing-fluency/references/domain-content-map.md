## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the adaptive shortcut systems whose dominance in low-effort decision contexts defines how consumers actually encounter advertising: the heuristic reframe (the Gigerenzer ecological-rationality position where fast-and-frugal heuristics often outperform elaborate analysis under real-world uncertainty, time pressure, and information overload, making shortcut use intelligent rather than deficient per the adaptive-tools literatures; the tension with the biases program where the same machinery reads as efficient heuristic or systematic bias depending on environment fit, a distinction that determines whether an advertiser designs to assist judgment or exploits mismatch; the recognition heuristic where known options get chosen over unknown ones in domains where recognition correlates with quality, the foundational mechanism of brand awareness investment); the consumer shortcut catalog (the brand heuristic where established names substitute for attribute evaluation in crowded categories, explaining why awareness and consistency compound into market advantage; the price-quality heuristic where cost signals quality in experience and credence goods including cosmetics, wine, and professional services, with the documented placebo-style effect of price on perceived performance; the social shortcut where review counts, ratings, and purchase volumes answer the quality question without investigation per the social-proof literatures of the following section; the effort heuristic where ease of completion gets read as signal of product and company quality, making friction itself persuasive evidence); the fluency effects system (the processing-fluency principle where stimuli that are easier to perceive, read, pronounce, and recall get judged as more familiar, more true, more likable, and less risky per the fluency research traditions; the perceptual-fluency levers including contrast, font legibility, visual clarity, and rhyme whose measurable evaluation effects survive consumer awareness; the conceptual-fluency levers including categorical fit, metaphor comprehension, and message coherence where ads that parse instantly outperform clever constructions requiring re-reading; the mere-exposure effect where repeated neutral contact breeds preference, the mechanism behind frequency-based media strategy and its wear-out boundary per the advertising-repetition literatures); involvement and processing depth (the elaboration continuum where purchase importance, personal relevance, and category knowledge determine how much scrutiny a message receives per the elaboration likelihood tradition; the peripheral-route reality where most ad exposure processes through cues including endorser attractiveness, production quality, music, and sheer repetition rather than argument strength; the central-route conditions where high-involvement contexts including expensive, technical, or identity-relevant purchases finally reward substantive argumentation; the strategy-matching discipline where creative execution mode must fit the audience's actual processing depth rather than the planner's hoped-for engagement); the habit and automaticity layer (the purchase-habit formation where repeated cued behavior escapes evaluation entirely, making shelf position, subscription defaults, and reorder reminders more powerful than any message per the habit literatures; the cue-routine-reward architecture where context triggers including time, place, emotional state, and preceding action govern automatic purchase behavior; the habit-disruption windows where life changes, stockouts, and relocations temporarily reopen automatic choices to persuasion per the behavioral-change research); and the fluency-design practice (the clarity-first audit where every campaign element faces the question of whether it processes instantly under distraction; the consistency discipline where uniform visual identity, verbal tone, and offer structure across touchpoints compounds fluency into familiarity into preference; the cognitive-load management where option counts, message claims, and form fields stay within working-memory limits per the choice-architecture section ahead) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the cleverness-prioritizing copywriter whose award-seeking complexity costs fluency and with it preference, remediated by the clarity-first audit; the deliberation-assuming strategist whose brief expects attribute comparison from consumers operating on recognition and price-quality shortcuts, remediated by the involvement continuum; the inconsistency-tolerant brand manager whose rotating palettes, voices, and offer styles rebuild familiarity from zero each quarter, remediated by the consistency discipline; the frequency-brute whose repetition investment passes the wear-out point into irritation and ad avoidance, remediated by the mere-exposure boundary literacy; and the friction-ignorant funnel designer whose checkout difficulty signals untrustworthiness while abandoning carts, remediated by the effort-heuristic understanding with detection methods as the diagnostic.
