## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the ethics and compliance boundary whose violation converts behavioral expertise into brand and legal liability, the discipline that determines whether the toolkit builds trust or extracts it: the normative framework (the persuasion-manipulation distinction where identical mechanics differ by transparency, consumer benefit, and reversibility — nudges withstand public disclosure while manipulations depend on ignorance per the publicity-principle traditions; the autonomy-respect standard where influence preserving genuine choice and informed consent separates ethical practice from overriding consumer judgment per the autonomy literatures; the welfare-test discipline where interventions get evaluated by whether the influenced party would endorse the influence with full information per the nudge-ethics traditions; the long-term-interest alignment where ethical practice and commercial sustainability converge because trust destruction through manipulation eventually prices into churn, chargebacks, and review damage per the trust-economics literatures); the dark-patterns taxonomy (the Brignull classification system naming the deceptive-design species including confirmshaming where opt-out language weaponizes guilt, roach-motel obstruction where entry is easy and exit difficult, forced continuity where trials silently convert to billing, hidden costs surfacing late in checkout, misdirection steering attention from unwanted options, trick questions in forms, bait-and-switch where action produces unexpected result, nagging repeated pressure after refusal, and sneak-into-cart preselection per the dark-pattern literatures; the FTC staff-report framework organizing patterns into design-based, content-based, and interface-based deception categories with enforcement precedent establishing legal exposure per the regulatory documents; the interface-manipulation mechanics where visual hierarchy, button design, and flow structure execute the patterns, making pattern literacy a design-review skill per the interface-ethics traditions); the regulatory landscape (the unfair-and-deceptive-practices foundation where Section 5 of the FTC Act and state analogs reach misleading scarcity claims, fake reviews, fabricated testimonials, and deceptive pricing per the enforcement traditions; the negative-option and subscription rules including ROSCA and the click-to-cancel requirements mandating clear consent, easy cancellation, and renewal disclosure per the subscription-enforcement frameworks; the review-and-endorsement rules requiring disclosure of material connections and prohibiting fake or suppressed reviews per the endorsement guides; the privacy-and-targeting regulation where behavioral advertising faces consent requirements under GDPR, CCPA, and successor frameworks with the platform-policy overlay of evolving targeting restrictions per the privacy literatures; the sector-specific regimes where advertising to children, health claims, financial products, alcohol, and gambling carry heightened behavioral-technique restrictions per the sector codes); the vulnerability and fairness dimension (the vulnerable-consumer framework where children, elderly, financially distressed, and addiction-affected populations face heightened exploitation risk from pressure techniques per the vulnerability literatures; the personalization-ethics boundary where behavioral targeting using intimate inference — health status, financial distress, life crisis — crosses into manipulation territory per the profiling-ethics discussions; the distributional-awareness principle where dark patterns concentrate harm on least-sophisticated consumers, an equity dimension regulators increasingly cite per the consumer-justice literatures); the professional-practice systems (the code-and-standard ecosystem including ANA, IPA, and platform policies whose behavioral-design provisions define industry expectation per the professional codes; the ethics-review integration where campaign approval workflows include behavioral-technique review with documented rationale per the governance traditions; the disclosure-by-design practice where material terms, real scarcity, genuine proof, and honest pricing survive full public description, the operational transparency test; the whistleblowing-and-culture dimension where team norms determine whether ethical concerns surface or normalize per the ethical-culture literatures); and the strategic-ethics case (the brand-trust-asset argument where sustained honesty compounds into proof generation, retention, and pricing power that manipulation cannot buy per the brand-equity traditions; the enforcement-risk-pricing where dark-pattern revenue gains face expected costs of fines, platform bans, litigation, and press exposure per the risk frameworks; the competitive-differentiation opportunity where visible ethical practice becomes positioning in sophistication-saturated markets per the trust-positioning literatures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the metric-drift operator whose conversion optimization incrementally adopts patterns nobody approved in aggregate, arriving at a roach-motel funnel through a hundred small decisions, remediated by the review-integration discipline; the everyone-does-it normalizer whose team culture treats dark patterns as standard craft until enforcement or exposure reclassifies them, remediated by the culture and code literacies; the disclosure-burier whose material terms hide in footnotes and second screens, converting legal compliance into technical fiction, remediated by the disclosure-by-design practice; the vulnerability-blind targeter whose pressure campaigns reach distressed populations through crisis-keyword targeting, remediated by the vulnerability framework; and the short-term-metric-tyrant whose quarterly conversion goals systematically discount trust assets that compound slowly, remediated by the strategic-ethics accounting with detection methods as the diagnostic.
