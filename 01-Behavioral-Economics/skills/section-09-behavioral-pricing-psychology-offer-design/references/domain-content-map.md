## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the price-perception discipline whose psychological structure of numbers, references, and payment mechanics determines whether the ask converts: the price-as-signal foundation (the dual-role principle where price functions simultaneously as economic cost and psychological signal of quality, exclusivity, and identity, with the signal reading varying by category knowledge and involvement per the price-perception literatures; the reference-price architecture where internal references from memory and experience combine with external references from displayed comparisons to determine whether an actual price reads as gain or loss per the reference-dependence of the prospect-theory section; the Weber-Fechner and just-noticeable-difference principles where price perception operates on proportions rather than absolutes, making identical dollar changes feel different across price points per the psychophysical traditions); the number-psychology toolkit (the charm-pricing effect where prices ending in nine carry documented demand advantages beyond rational rounding, associated with value positioning and deal perception per the price-ending literatures; the left-digit effect where first-digit processing makes twenty-nine-ninety-nine read as closer to twenty than thirty per the encoding research; the precision effect where exact-looking prices signal calculated value and fair dealing while round numbers signal quality and luxury, a positioning lever per the precision literatures; the digit-display conventions where font size, syllable count, and visual presentation modulate magnitude perception per the fluency connections of the heuristic section); the price-architecture engineering (the anchor-placement craft where highest-price-first presentation and premium-option display shift willingness to pay for the full set per the anchoring literatures; the tier-and-decoy integration where the option-set mechanics of the choice-architecture section apply specifically to subscription tables and product ladders with the compromise-effect targeting; the bundling logic where combined pricing obscures component evaluation and raises perceived value through the mental-accounting dynamics of combined versus segregated gains per the Thaler traditions; the partitioned-pricing discipline where base-plus-fee structures lower headline anchors but accumulate drip-pricing resentment and regulatory exposure under total-price-transparency enforcement per the fee-disclosure rules; the discount-architecture craft where percent-off versus amount-off framing follows the rule-of-one-hundred logic — percentages dominate under a hundred, amounts above — per the discount-framing literatures); the payment-psychology layer (the pain-of-paying concept where transaction salience creates negative affect that payment mechanics can amplify or mute, cash hurting most and frictionless one-click hurting least per the payment-decoupling literatures; the subscription-invisibility effect where recurring billing escapes per-purchase evaluation, powering retention while attracting click-to-cancel regulatory response per the negative-option enforcement frameworks; the prepaid-and-sunk dynamics where advance payment converts future consumption into free-feeling usage per the mental-accounting traditions; the installment-and-deferral effects where buy-now-pay-later structures reframe affordability with the overconsumption concerns drawing regulatory attention per the BNPL literatures); the promotion-psychology discipline (the deal-proneness segmentation where promotion-responsive consumers differ systematically from full-price buyers, informing targeted discount strategy per the promotion literatures; the quality-dilution dynamic where deep frequent discounting trains reference prices downward and erodes brand signal, the promotion-addiction trap per the brand-equity research; the reason-for-discount practice where justified promotions — seasonal, clearance, anniversary — protect reference prices against arbitrary-devaluation reading per the justification literatures; the coupon-and-code psychology where effort-requiring discounts raise redemption commitment and segment price-sensitive demand per the couponing traditions); and the offer-construction synthesis (the complete-offer concept where price, terms, guarantee, bonus, and payment structure combine into a single prospect evaluated against references per the prospect-integration traditions; the risk-reversal craft where guarantees, trials, and return policies price the consumer's loss aversion directly, converting hesitation into endowment per the loss-aversion section; the offer-testing discipline where price points and structures face the experimentation standards of the measurement section with attention to long-term reference-price effects beyond immediate conversion) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the signal-conflict pricer whose luxury positioning carries charm-ending value pricing or whose value brand displays round-number premium signals, remediated by the dual-role literacy; the drip-pricer whose partitioned fees convert checkout wins into chargebacks and enforcement exposure, remediated by the transparency discipline; the discount-addict whose promotion calendar has trained the audience to never pay full price, collapsing margin and brand signal, remediated by the dilution-awareness practice; the anchor-orphan whose price displays lack the comparison structure that makes anchoring work, remediated by the architecture engineering; and the pain-maximizer whose payment flows amplify transaction salience in contexts where decoupling serves retention, remediated by the payment-psychology layer with detection methods as the diagnostic.
