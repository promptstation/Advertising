## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the decision-under-risk theory whose loss asymmetry and reference dependence generate the most commercially powerful levers in behavioral advertising: the expected-utility failure (the classical model where consumers evaluate outcomes by final wealth and objective probabilities, versus the documented violations including the Allais paradox patterns, certainty effects, and preference reversals that motivated a new theory per the decision-research traditions; the stakes for advertising where offers, guarantees, and price communications implicitly ask consumers to evaluate prospects, making the actual evaluation function a design input rather than an academic curiosity); the prospect-theory architecture (the reference-dependence principle where outcomes get coded as gains or losses relative to an expectation anchor rather than evaluated absolutely, making the reference point itself — current price, competitor price, suggested retail, past experience — a manipulable campaign variable; the value function where losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains please, with diminishing sensitivity on both sides producing the S-shaped curve whose steepness ratio is among the most replicated findings in the field per the loss-aversion literatures; the probability-weighting function where people overweight small probabilities and underweight moderate-to-large ones, explaining lottery-ticket purchases and extended-warranty uptake simultaneously; the certainty effect where guaranteed outcomes receive disproportionate weight against probabilistic ones of higher expected value, shaping preference for fixed discounts over chance-based promotions); the loss-aversion application catalog (the endowment effect where ownership, even imagined or trial-based, raises valuation and reluctance to give up, the mechanism behind free trials, test drives, home try-on programs, and money-back guarantees that convert by making return feel like loss; the status-quo bias where current arrangements resist change beyond what switching costs justify, favoring incumbents and making default options powerful per the nudge literatures of the choice-architecture section; the sunk-cost pattern where prior investment in subscriptions, loyalty programs, and compatibility ecosystems continues to drive retention beyond forward-looking value; the omission-bias and regret-aversion dynamics where consumers fear action-caused losses more than inaction-caused ones, a barrier that loss-framed urgency and social-proof reassurance address per the regret literatures); the framing methodology (the gain-frame versus loss-frame decision where identical objective outcomes communicate as securing benefit or avoiding harm, with the documented finding that loss frames dominate for prevention-oriented behaviors and detection contexts while gain frames suit promotion-oriented aspirations per the framing-effect literatures; the attribute-framing craft where lean meat at ninety percent fat-free outsells ten percent fat, the same fact carrying different valence per the classic framing experiments; the price-frame engineering where reference-price presentation, discount anchoring, installment partitioning, and bundling restructure the prospect consumers actually evaluate per the pricing-psychology section ahead; the message-tone calibration where loss appeals carry arousal and attention advantages but accumulate reactance, fear, and brand-damage risks at high intensity, requiring the calibration discipline documented in fear-appeal research); the ethical and regulatory perimeter (the artificial-loss prohibition zone where fabricated scarcity, fake countdown timers, and phantom reference prices constitute deception under consumer-protection enforcement rather than legitimate framing per the FTC dark-patterns taxonomy; the vulnerability-awareness principle where loss-framing pressure applied to financially strained, elderly, or distressed consumers crosses ethical lines independent of legality per the consumer-vulnerability literatures; the transparency standard where frames remain honest presentations of real prospects rather than manufactured ones, the line separating persuasion craft from fraud); and the theory-integration practice (the combined-mechanism design where loss aversion operates alongside social proof, scarcity, and defaults within coherent offer structures rather than isolated tactics; the measurement discipline where framed-message tests follow the experimentation standards of the measurement section with attention to effect-size realism across categories) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the gain-frame-monotone marketer whose aspirational-only messaging forfeits the stronger motivational pull of loss aversion in prevention contexts, remediated by the framing methodology; the fake-scarcity operator whose manufactured urgency draws enforcement action and review-site backlash that outlasts the campaign, remediated by the perimeter discipline; the reference-point-drifter whose price communications ignore what consumers actually anchor to, comparing premium offers against the wrong baseline, remediated by the reference-dependence literacy; the loss-frame-bludgeoner whose every touchpoint threatens loss until brand association becomes anxiety, remediated by the tone-calibration discipline; and the probability-weighting-ignorer whose guarantee and insurance-style offer design misses that consumers overweight small risks, leaving conversion value on the table, remediated by the weighting-function application with detection methods as the diagnostic.
