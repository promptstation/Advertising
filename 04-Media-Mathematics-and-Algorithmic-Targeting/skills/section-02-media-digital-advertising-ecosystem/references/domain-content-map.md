## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Maps the industry structure whose players, incentives, and power asymmetries determine how media dollars flow and where measurement truth resides: the participant landscape (the advertiser-and-agency structure where in-house teams, holding-company agencies, and specialist boutiques differ in capability, incentive, and accountability per the industry-structure literatures; the demand-side-platform role where buying technology aggregates advertiser demand across exchanges with the independence-versus-platform-owned distinction that shapes data access and incentive alignment per the DSP literatures; the supply-side-platform role where publisher yield management aggregates inventory with the reseller-chain complexity documented per the SSP literatures; the exchange-function where auction infrastructure matches demand and supply in milliseconds per the exchange literatures of the programmatic section; the ad-network-and-marketplace layer where curated and managed inventory channels serve buyers outside pure auction flows per the network traditions; the walled-garden reality where the major social, search, and retail platforms operate closed ecosystems whose auction rules, data access, and measurement they unilaterally define per the platform literatures; the verification-and-measurement-industry where third parties audit viewability, fraud, and brand safety with the accreditation standards of the MRC traditions per the verification literatures); the incentive-and-power analysis (the conflict-mapping discipline where every participant's revenue model — percentage of spend, arbitrage spread, data monetization — predicts behavior including the transparency studies' documented principal-agent problems per the ISBA and ANA findings of the sources; the data-asymmetry reality where platforms see cross-advertiser signals that buyers never access, making optimization recommendations partially self-interested per the asymmetry literatures; the scale-power-dynamics where the largest platforms set market terms including measurement standards, privacy architecture, and fee structures that the industry adapts to per the platform-power literatures; the principal-agent-structure audit where advertiser-agency-platform relationships carry disclosed and undisclosed compensation whose alignment determines advice quality per the transparency traditions); the technology-stack literacy (the martech-stack-architecture where campaign management, data platforms, creative systems, measurement tools, and automation layers integrate with the integration-quality variance documented per the stack literatures; the API-and-data-access-tier reality where platform data availability differs by spend level and partnership status, structuring measurement capability per the access literatures; the identity-and-data-infrastructure layer where identity graphs, customer data platforms, and clean rooms operate per the privacy-era frameworks of the analytics companion; the measurement-stack-composition where platform analytics, attribution vendors, MMM tools, and experimentation services combine per the triad traditions of the analytics companion); the channel-portfolio overview (the search-channel economics where intent-driven auctions dominate commercial queries per the search section ahead; the social-channel economics where attention-driven feeds monetize engagement per the algorithms section; the display-and-video-programmatic layer whose open-exchange dynamics the following sections dissect; the retail-media emergence where commerce platforms monetize purchase-intent inventory per the emerging-channels section; the audio, connected-TV, and out-of-home digitization where traditional channels adopt programmatic mechanics per the emerging-channels section); and the ecosystem-navigation practice (the vendor-evaluation discipline where technology partners face assessment of incentive alignment, data portability, measurement independence, and contract terms per the procurement literatures; the platform-diversification judgment where channel concentration risks versus operational efficiency balance per the portfolio traditions; the industry-knowledge-maintenance where policy changes, product launches, and enforcement actions require continuous tracking per the currency traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the incentive-blind-buyer whose platform account-team recommendations face no conflict-of-interest discount, remediated by the incentive-mapping discipline; the walled-garden-surrender strategist whose entire measurement truth rests on platform self-reporting with no independent check, remediated by the asymmetry literacy; the stack-spaghetti-operator whose unintegrated tools produce conflicting numbers nobody arbitrates, remediated by the architecture literacy; the channel-monoculturist whose single-platform dependence inherits every algorithm change and policy shift as existential risk, remediated by the diversification judgment; the vendor-lock-in-ignorer whose data and workflow captivity prevents exit at contract renewal, remediated by the evaluation discipline; and the ecosystem-static-assumer whose three-year-old industry map misses the retail-media and privacy transformations reshaping power, remediated by the knowledge-maintenance practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
