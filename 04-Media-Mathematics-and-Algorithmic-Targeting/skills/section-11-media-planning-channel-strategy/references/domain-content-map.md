## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the planning discipline where objectives, budgets, and channel mechanics assemble into executable strategy, integrating every prior section into professional media work: the planning-process foundation (the brief-to-plan translation where business objectives, audience definitions, budget constraints, and timing requirements convert into media objectives with measurable targets per the planning literatures; the objective-specification discipline where reach, frequency, traffic, conversion, and efficiency targets get quantified with baselines before channel selection per the objective traditions; the audience-definition-integration where target segments from the analytics companion's clustering and the targeting section's audience engineering establish the planning population per the audience literatures; the constraint-mapping practice where brand-safety requirements, regulatory limits, platform policies, and measurement availability bound feasible plans per the constraint traditions); the reach-and-frequency planning (the audience-flow-modeling where platform reach curves, duplication analysis, and frequency distributions get projected with the digital-data-availability caveats per the planning traditions of the mathematics section; the recency-versus-continuity debate where concentrated burst and continuous presence strategies suit different purchase cycles and category dynamics per the scheduling literatures; the flighting-architecture where continuous, pulsing, and seasonal patterns structure timing with the auction-cost seasonality documented per the scheduling traditions; the effective-frequency-application where message-processing thresholds from the behavioral companion inform minimum exposure targets per the frequency literatures of the mathematics section); the channel-portfolio construction (the funnel-role-allocation where demand-creation channels building awareness and association differ from demand-capture channels converting existing intent, with the balance research suggesting creation-heavy allocation for long-term growth per the effectiveness literatures connecting to the analytics companion's attribution frameworks; the channel-selection-criteria where audience presence, intent quality, creative-format fit, cost efficiency, measurement capability, and competitive dynamics structure evaluation per the selection traditions; the cross-channel-synergy-mapping where search volume responds to social exposure, display supports video, and retail media captures created demand, the interaction effects whose measurement the incrementality frameworks address per the synergy literatures; the diversification-versus-concentration-judgment where platform risk, algorithm volatility, and operational capacity balance against focus efficiency per the portfolio traditions of the ecosystem section); the budget-allocation methodology (the top-down-versus-bottom-up-reconciliation where strategic allocation targets meet channel-level feasibility checks per the allocation traditions; the response-curve-informed-allocation where diminishing-returns modeling from the analytics companion's MMM and forecasting systems guides distribution per the optimization literatures; the test-and-learn-reserve discipline where experimentation budgets fund the incrementality programs per the experimentation traditions of the analytics companion; the contingency-and-flexibility-management where reallocation mechanisms respond to performance, competitive, and market shifts per the flexibility traditions); the plan-documentation-and-communication practice (the media-plan-architecture where channel allocations, flighting calendars, targeting specifications, creative requirements, measurement plans, and assumptions persist as executable documents per the documentation traditions; the assumption-transparency-discipline where every projection states its basis because plans get judged later against forecasts whose confidence intervals nobody recorded per the transparency traditions; the stakeholder-presentation-craft where plans communicate strategy logic to clients and leadership using the storytelling standards of the analytics companion per the presentation traditions; the plan-to-execution-handoff where trading teams receive specifications complete enough for faithful implementation per the operations traditions); and the performance-management cycle (the in-flight-optimization-governance where reallocation decisions follow documented thresholds and rationale rather than daily dashboard reactions per the governance traditions of the bidding section; the readout-and-learning-integration where campaign results feed the insight repositories and planning benchmarks per the readout traditions of the analytics companion; the planning-benchmark-accumulation where historical channel performance, cost trends, and response patterns build the institutional priors that improve future plans per the benchmark traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the channel-first-planner whose favorite platforms select strategy before objectives and audience analysis occur, remediated by the brief-to-plan discipline; the capture-heavy-allocator whose search and retargeting concentration harvests demand nobody funded creation of, eroding future volume, remediated by the funnel-role research; the assumption-hidden-projector whose reach and cost forecasts circulate without stated bases, making accountability impossible, remediated by the transparency discipline; the rigid-plan-defender whose reallocation governance prevents response to obvious in-flight evidence, remediated by the flexibility management; the daily-dashboard-reactor whose hourly optimizations violate learning phases and strategic intent, remediated by the optimization governance; and the readout-free-planner whose campaigns never produce accumulated benchmarks, restarting every plan from industry-average guesswork, remediated by the learning integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
