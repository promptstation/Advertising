## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Establishes the quantitative language of media buying whose fluency separates professionals who defend budgets with arithmetic from those who defend them with dashboard screenshots: the core-rate vocabulary (the cost-per-thousand-impressions foundation where CPM anchors display and video economics with the impression-definition scrutiny that determines what a thousand impressions actually contains per the measurement-standards literatures; the performance-rate family where cost-per-click, cost-per-engagement, cost-per-action, and cost-per-acquisition express efficiency across funnel stages with the conversion-definition dependency that makes identical acronyms incomparable across platforms per the rate literatures; the earned-versus-paid distinctions where organic reach, engagement rates, and follower metrics interact with paid performance in blended economics per the channel literatures; the viewability-and-attention refinement where served impressions diverge from viewable impressions diverge from attentive exposure, the measurement hierarchy whose conflation inflates reported efficiency per the MRC-standards traditions of the verification section); the reach-and-frequency mathematics (the reach-frequency relationship where gross impressions distribute across unique audience with the frequency-curve mechanics of repetition per the planning traditions; the effective-frequency literature where minimum exposure thresholds for message processing and the wear-out ceilings connect to the mere-exposure dynamics of the behavioral companion course; the rating-point system where gross rating points and target-rating points aggregate reach and frequency for planning with the digital-adaptation challenges documented per the ratings traditions; the duplication-and-overlap algebra where multi-channel audiences intersect, making naive reach addition mathematically wrong per the overlap literatures); the budget-and-efficiency arithmetic (the working-media-versus-fee distinction where agency fees, technology costs, production, and data expenses consume budget before any impression buys, the split whose opacity the supply-chain section addresses per the transparency studies of the sources; the budget-pacing mathematics where daily and lifetime budgets distribute across flight periods with the auction-competition seasonality that makes uniform pacing naive per the pacing literatures of the bidding section; the diminishing-returns reality where audience expansion and frequency increases face rising marginal costs and falling response, connecting media math to the response curves of the analytics companion course; the break-even-computation practice where allowable cost-per-acquisition derives from customer value and margin per the unit-economics traditions of the analytics companion); the funnel-mathematics system (the conversion-rate-algebra where funnel stage rates multiply into end-to-end efficiency, making single-stage optimization potentially counterproductive per the funnel literatures; the volume-quality-tradeoff mechanics where targeting relaxation raises volume while lowering rates, with the marginal-cost analysis determining optimal breadth per the optimization traditions; the attribution-window-mathematics where conversion counting periods alter reported performance systematically per the measurement literatures of the analytics companion; the incrementality-adjusted-efficiency concept where true cost-per-incremental-conversion exceeds reported cost per the causal frameworks of the analytics companion); the cross-channel-comparability discipline (the metric-definition-audit practice where impressions, clicks, and conversions get reconciled across platforms whose definitions differ in bot filtering, click types, and counting rules per the definitional literatures; the apples-to-oranges-guard discipline where CPM comparisons across channels ignore attention quality, viewability rates, and context differences unless adjusted per the comparability traditions; the blended-economics-modeling where multi-channel campaigns compute combined efficiency with proper weighting per the planning traditions of the strategy section); and the professional-numeracy habits (the back-of-envelope fluency where media professionals estimate costs, reach, and efficiency mentally within factor-of-two accuracy before any spreadsheet; the unit-consistency discipline where rates, currencies, periods, and populations stay coherent through multi-step calculations; the sanity-check reflex where reported numbers face magnitude plausibility against known benchmarks before acceptance per the skepticism traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the impression-equality-assumer whose CPM optimization buys thousand-units of unviewed, fraudulent, or adjacent-to-nothing inventory at bargain rates, remediated by the viewability hierarchy; the reach-addition-error planner whose multi-channel totals double-count overlapping audiences, inflating reported coverage, remediated by the overlap algebra; the fee-blind-budgeter whose media plan never distinguishes working dollars from intermediation costs, remediated by the working-media distinction; the single-stage-optimizer whose click-rate improvements destroy end-to-end efficiency through quality dilution, remediated by the funnel mathematics; the cross-platform-comparator whose rate tables ignore definitional incompatibility, producing meaningless rankings, remediated by the comparability discipline; and the numeracy-dependent-on-tools analyst who cannot sanity-check a media plan without opening a spreadsheet, remediated by the professional habits with detection methods as the diagnostic.
