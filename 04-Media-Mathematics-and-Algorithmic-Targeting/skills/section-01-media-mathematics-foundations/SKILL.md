# Media Mathematics Foundations: Metrics, Rates, and the Economics of Attention — Professional Training

## Mission

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Platform-report credulity: accepting self-reported impressions, conversions, and lift from platforms grading their own homework without independent verification or triangulation; Vanity-reach buying: optimizing for cheap CPMs that accumulate invalid traffic, made-for-advertising sites, and unseen impressions while genuine reach collapses; Smart-bidding black-box surrender: handing budget and signal authority to automated bidding without understanding what conversion events feed the machine or how learning phases behave; Supply-chain ignorance: never tracing where media dollars actually land — intermediaries, reseller markups, and arbitrage paths consuming working media silently; Privacy-era denial: building targeting and measurement plans on deprecated identifiers and tracking assumptions that regulation and platform policy have already invalidated; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
