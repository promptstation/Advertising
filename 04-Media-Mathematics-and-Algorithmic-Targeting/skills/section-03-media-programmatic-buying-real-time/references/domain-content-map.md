## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the programmatic transaction machinery whose millisecond auctions allocate the majority of open display and video inventory, the engineering the course description names as essential understanding: the programmatic-concept foundation (the automation-principle where direct-sales negotiation gives way to machine-executed transactions at impression scale per the programmatic literatures; the transaction-type inventory spanning open auctions, private marketplaces, preferred deals, and programmatic-guaranteed arrangements whose control-price-access tradeoffs structure buying strategy per the deal-type literatures; the buy-side-operating-model where traders, algorithms, and automation layers execute strategy with the human-judgment allocation questions documented per the trading traditions); the real-time-bidding mechanics (the bid-request-lifecycle where page load triggers an auction request carrying page, user, and context signals broadcast to demand sources within the hundred-millisecond budget per the OpenRTB-protocol traditions of the sources; the bid-decision-computation where demand-side platforms evaluate requests against campaign targeting, audience data, predicted value, and budget state to produce bid prices per the DSP literatures; the auction-format-evolution where second-price auctions historically dominated before the industry shift to first-price mechanics changed bidding strategy fundamentally, making bid-shading algorithms necessary per the auction literatures; the win-notice-and-serving chain where auction results propagate to creative delivery with the discrepancy points between served, viewable, and counted impressions documented per the measurement literatures); the valuation-and-bidding theory (the expected-value-bidding principle where rational bids equal predicted conversion probability times conversion value adjusted for margin, the formula connecting the propensity models of the analytics companion to auction behavior per the bidding literatures; the bid-shading mechanics where first-price auctions reward algorithmic reduction of bids below value estimates with the transparency questions about shading behavior per the shading literatures; the win-rate-and-landscape dynamics where bid distributions determine impression access with the auction-pressure feedback of competition documented per the landscape literatures; the budget-constrained-optimization where pacing algorithms distribute spend across time and inventory to maximize value under budget limits per the pacing literatures of the bidding section); the targeting-signal-system (the audience-signal-taxonomy where first-party segments, marketplace data, contextual classification, and inferred attributes combine in bid decisions with the privacy-era availability constraints per the targeting section; the contextual-targeting renaissance where page-content classification regained strategic weight as identifier signals degraded per the contextual literatures; the signal-quality-hierarchy where deterministic identifiers, probabilistic inference, and contextual signals carry different precision and coverage tradeoffs per the identity literatures of the analytics companion); the supply-chain transparency (the reseller-chain reality where inventory passes through multiple intermediaries whose fees and markups the transparency studies quantified, with working-media shares documented as low as fractions of spend per the ISBA and ANA findings of the sources; the ads-txt-and-sellers-json infrastructure where authorization files and reseller disclosure standards enable chain auditing per the IAB-standards of the sources; the supply-chain-object-literacy where transaction metadata carries chain information whose analysis reveals actual dollar flow per the transparency literatures; the supply-path-optimization practice where buyers consolidate routes to inventory for efficiency, transparency, and quality per the SPO traditions of the exchange section); and the programmatic-operations practice (the campaign-structure discipline where flighting, frequency management, and inventory selection execute strategy within auction mechanics per the operations traditions; the quality-control-monitoring where invalid traffic, viewability, and brand-safety metrics receive continuous surveillance per the verification section; the troubleshooting-methodology where delivery failures, cost anomalies, and performance breaks receive systematic diagnosis through the request-to-serve chain per the operations literatures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the auction-ignorant-bidder whose value estimates ignore first-price mechanics and shading reality, systematically overpaying for impressions, remediated by the bidding theory; the deal-type-default-trader whose all-open-auction strategy forgoes private-marketplace quality and guaranteed delivery where they fit, remediated by the transaction inventory; the reseller-chain-blind-buyer whose spend traverses unauthorized and high-fee paths because authorization files never got audited, remediated by the transparency infrastructure; the signal-decay-denier whose audience-targeting strategy assumes identifier availability that platform and regulatory changes removed, remediated by the signal-hierarchy literacy; the set-and-forget-trader whose campaigns run unmonitored through fraud spikes and delivery failures until month-end reporting reveals disaster, remediated by the operations practice; and the black-box-acceptor whose DSP optimization recommendations face no mechanism understanding, delegating strategy to vendor algorithms with undisclosed objectives, remediated by the machinery literacy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
