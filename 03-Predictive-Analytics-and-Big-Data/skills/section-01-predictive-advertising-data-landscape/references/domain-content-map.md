## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Maps the data territory every predictive capability depends on, including the structural transformation that redefined the field: the data-source taxonomy (the first-party category where owned properties including websites, apps, CRM systems, loyalty programs, and direct registrations supply consented customer data whose richness and reliability make it the strategic core per the data-strategy literatures; the second-party category where partner data sharing through direct agreements extends reach with contractual control per the partnership traditions; the third-party category where aggregated external data from brokers, panels, and platforms historically powered audience targeting, now contracting under regulation and platform policy per the ecosystem literatures; the observed-behavior streams including clickstream, transaction, engagement, and location data whose volume constitutes the big-data reality the course description names; the market-signal sources spanning search trends, social conversation, review platforms, and competitor activity whose trend-detection value the later sections develop); the adtech-ecosystem architecture (the demand-and-supply structure where advertisers, demand-side platforms, ad exchanges, supply-side platforms, and publishers form the transaction chain whose data exhaust feeds analytics per the programmatic literatures connecting to the media-mathematics companion course; the measurement-vendor landscape where attribution providers, verification services, panel companies, and analytics platforms populate the tooling market with the fragmentation and overlap that demand procurement literacy; the identity-layer concept where cookies, device identifiers, hashed emails, and authenticated accounts connect observations across touchpoints, the technical foundation whose erosion drives the current transformation per the identity literatures); the privacy transformation (the regulation-driven shift where GDPR, CCPA, and successor frameworks established consent requirements, purpose limitation, and consumer rights that restructured permissible data collection per the regulatory texts; the platform-policy shift where browser cookie deprecation, app-tracking transparency, and platform signal restriction removed identifier foundations that a decade of analytics assumed per the platform literatures; the consent-management reality where preference centers, consent management platforms, and signal loss create incomplete data whose bias structure must be understood rather than ignored per the measurement-bias literatures; the strategic response landscape where zero-party data collection, contextual targeting revival, authenticated-ecosystem investment, and privacy-preserving aggregation including clean rooms and differential-privacy approaches constitute the post-cookie playbook per the industry-transition literatures); the data-economics and strategy discipline (the collection-cost-value framework where each data source faces evaluation of acquisition cost, coverage, accuracy, activation capability, and compliance risk per the data-economics traditions; the data-quality-preview literacy where coverage gaps, identity fragmentation, latency, and definitional inconsistency get assessed before any modeling begins per the quality frameworks of the following section; the competitive-data-landscape reading where category norms on data investment and measurement sophistication set the bar analytical capability must clear; the governance-foundation awareness where ownership, stewardship, access policy, and retention schedules determine whether data remains usable asset or becomes liability per the governance literatures of the quality section); and the analyst's orientation (the question-first-data-second discipline where analysis needs select sources rather than availability determining questions; the provenance-tracking habit where every dataset carries documentation of origin, collection method, processing history, and known limitations per the lineage traditions; the freshness-and-decay awareness where advertising data ages fast because audiences, platforms, and behaviors shift continuously per the drift literatures of the modeling sections) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the hoarder-syndrome collector whose lake accumulates every available feed while no decision waits on any analysis, remediated by the question-first discipline; the third-party-dependent strategist whose audience plans assume identifier availability that regulation and platforms already removed, remediated by the transformation literacy; the consent-blind-builder whose collection practices predate compliance review until enforcement or platform penalty forces rebuild, remediated by the governance foundation; the vendor-map-ignorant buyer whose overlapping measurement purchases produce conflicting numbers nobody arbitrates, remediated by the ecosystem-procurement literacy; the provenance-losing team whose datasets circulate without origin documentation until nobody can state what a number means, remediated by the tracking habit; and the staleness-blind modeler whose audiences and trends run on months-old data in a weekly-shifting market, remediated by the freshness awareness with detection methods as the diagnostic.
