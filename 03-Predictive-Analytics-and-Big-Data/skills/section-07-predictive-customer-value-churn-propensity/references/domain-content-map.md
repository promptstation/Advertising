## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the customer-analytics discipline where value, retention, and response models convert raw datasets into the relationship economics that justify advertising spend: the lifetime-value foundation (the CLV concept architecture where customer lifetime value aggregates expected future contribution across the relationship horizon, connecting acquisition cost to sustainable spend per the customer-equity literatures; the historical-versus-predictive distinction where past-value analysis describes while predictive CLV forecasts, with the modeling approaches spanning simple average-based estimates through probabilistic purchase-timing models including the buy-till-you-die family per the CLV literatures; the margin-and-cost discipline where revenue-based value claims collapse under contribution-margin, service-cost, and acquisition-cost reality, demanding the full-economics view per the unit-economics traditions; the cohort-analysis method where entry-period groups tracked over time reveal value trajectories, retention curves, and payback periods whose patterns inform budget allocation per the cohort literatures); the churn-prediction system (the churn-definition craft where the absence-of-transaction problem requires explicit inactivity windows and category-appropriate definitions because subscription, retail, and B2B churn mean different things per the retention literatures; the churn-signal features where usage decay, engagement frequency, support contacts, payment failures, and competitive exposure predict departure with documented lead times per the churn literatures; the survival-analysis methods where time-to-event modeling handles censored customers still active at observation end, the statistically correct treatment most dashboard churn rates ignore per the survival traditions; the save-economics integration where churn probability times customer value times save-offer cost determines intervention targeting, converting prediction into profitable action per the retention-economics traditions); the propensity-application suite (the response-propensity systems where purchase-likelihood scoring drives campaign targeting and suppression with the incrementality caution that high-propensity customers often convert without advertising per the uplift literatures of the modeling section; the offer-sensitivity modeling where discount responsiveness prediction enables margin-protecting promotion targeting per the promotion literatures of the behavioral companion; the next-product-and-channel prediction where recommendation and channel-preference models drive personalization per the recommendation traditions; the fraud-and-abuse detection where anomaly patterns in claims, redemptions, and traffic protect marketing budgets per the detection literatures); the value-based-media integration (the acquisition-economics connection where predicted CLV sets allowable cost-per-acquisition by segment, the bridge from customer analytics to media investment per the efficiency literatures of the media companion; the value-optimized-bidding reality where platform algorithms trained on conversion-value signals shift delivery toward high-value users with the signal-quality and privacy-era data dependencies documented per the bidding literatures; the audience-tiering practice where value and propensity scores stratify reach strategies across retention, cross-sell, and prospecting objectives per the planning traditions); the measurement-and-validation discipline (the prediction-versus-outcome tracking where model forecasts face realized behavior with the calibration and drift monitoring of the modeling section applied to relationship metrics; the incrementality-testing requirement where retention and acquisition interventions prove causal lift rather than claiming credit for inevitable behavior per the experimentation literatures of the behavioral companion; the payback-and-horizon analysis where value recovery timing determines cash-flow-constrained budget decisions per the financial literatures; the segment-economics-disaggregation where aggregate CLV hides profitable and unprofitable customer mixes demanding segment-level accounting per the disaggregation traditions); and the privacy-era adaptation (the signal-degradation reality where tracking loss compresses observation windows and identity coverage, requiring modeling adjustments including stronger first-party dependence and cohort-level estimation per the transformation literatures of the landscape section; the consent-population-bias management where consented users differ systematically from non-consenters, biasing value models unless addressed per the missingness frameworks of the quality section) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the revenue-CLV-promoter whose value models ignore margin and service costs, justifying acquisition spend that loses money per customer, remediated by the economics discipline; the arbitrary-window-churn-definer whose inactivity thresholds create churn statistics nobody can act on consistently, remediated by the definition craft; the propensity-only-targeter whose high-score audiences convert organically while advertising pays for inevitable purchases, remediated by the incrementality requirement; the dashboard-survival-ignorant whose naive churn rates misread censored customers still in relationship, remediated by the survival methods; the aggregate-value-hiding analyst whose blended CLV masks unprofitable segments absorbing acquisition budget, remediated by the disaggregation discipline; and the signal-degradation-denier whose models trained on pre-privacy data face degraded inputs without retraining or redesign, remediated by the adaptation practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
