Establish data trust through the quality framework (accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity dimensions), assurance practice (profiling audits, automated testing, anomaly monitoring, root-cause triage), governance architecture (ownership stewardship, catalogs dictionaries, policy frameworks, metrics governance), privacy compliance (legal-basis mapping, consent architecture, rights operations, purpose limitation, vendor management), privacy engineering (aggregation thresholds, clean rooms, differential privacy.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the quality and compliance discipline whose absence makes every downstream model and forecast untrustworthy regardless of statistical sophistication: the quality-dimension framework (the accuracy dimension where values reflect reality, threatened by tracking errors, bot traffic, attribution mistakes, and entry failures per the quality literatures; the completeness dimension where missing records, consent-filtered populations, and coverage gaps bias every statistic computed on the remainder per the missingness literatures whose mechanism taxonomy determines whether deletion, imputation, or explicit bias acknowledgment applies; the consistency dimension where the same entity, event, or metric carries different values across systems until reconciliation rules establish authority per the consistency traditions; the timeliness dimension where data arrives late enough to miss decisions, the freshness discipline of the landscape section operationalized through latency monitoring; the validity-and-integrity dimensions where schema violations, orphan records, and broken referential chains corrupt joins per the integrity traditions); the quality-assurance practice (the profiling-and-audit methodology where new sources face distribution checks, null rates, duplicate analysis, and cross-source reconciliation before production use per the profiling traditions; the automated-testing discipline where pipeline outputs face assertion tests on volume, ranges, uniqueness, and referential integrity with failure alerts per the data-testing literatures; the anomaly-detection monitoring where statistical process control on key metrics catches collection failures that silent-corruption otherwise hides per the monitoring traditions; the root-cause-triage practice where quality incidents get diagnosed through lineage tracing to source, transformation, or definition failure per the incident traditions); the governance architecture (the ownership-and-stewardship model where every dataset and metric carries accountable owners with defined decision rights per the governance literatures; the catalog-and-dictionary systems where discoverable metadata prevents the reinvention and misinterpretation cycles of ungoverned estates per the catalog traditions; the policy-framework discipline where retention schedules, access tiers, usage restrictions, and classification standards codify permissible data handling per the policy literatures; the metrics-governance practice where centralized definition authority ends the dashboard-conflict crisis per the aggregation governance of the infrastructure section); the privacy-compliance system (the legal-basis mapping where consent, legitimate interest, contract, and other bases get matched to collection and processing activities per the GDPR and CCPA frameworks; the consent-signal-architecture where preference management platforms propagate consent states into collection and processing systems, the technical execution of legal requirement per the consent literatures; the data-subject-rights operations where access, deletion, portability, and opt-out requests flow through systems within regulatory deadlines per the rights-fulfillment traditions; the purpose-limitation discipline where data collected for analytics cannot silently extend into other uses without basis review per the limitation principles; the cross-border-and-vendor-management rules where processor agreements, transfer mechanisms, and vendor audits extend compliance through the supply chain per the third-party-risk traditions); the privacy-engineering frontier (the aggregation-and-threshold techniques where group-level reporting with minimum-cell sizes limits re-identification per the disclosure-control traditions; the clean-room paradigm where advertisers and platforms compute on combined data without exposing raw records, the dominant collaboration architecture of the privacy era per the clean-room literatures; the differential-privacy and noise-addition approaches where mathematical privacy guarantees trade precision for protection per the privacy-technology literatures; the privacy-by-design integration where compliance shapes system architecture from conception rather than constraining completed builds per the design traditions); and the trust-economics perspective (the quality-value connection where decision quality inherits data quality, making quality investment measurable in avoided bad-decision cost per the information-economics traditions; the brand-trust-dimension where consumer data handling constitutes brand experience whose violations surface as churn and reputation damage per the trust literatures of the behavioral companion course; the regulatory-risk-pricing where fine exposure, platform penalties, and litigation risk quantify compliance negligence per the risk traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the garbage-in-modeler whose sophisticated algorithms process unaudited sources, producing precise answers to corrupted questions, remediated by the profiling discipline; the silent-corruption victim whose tracking failure runs undetected for weeks because no volume or distribution monitoring existed, remediated by the detection systems; the consent-decoupled collector whose preference states never propagate into processing systems, making legal consent technically fictional, remediated by the signal architecture; the governance-theater performer whose policy documents exist while ownership, catalogs, and definition authority remain unexercised, remediated by the architecture practice; the deletion-blind-retainer whose indefinite data hoarding violates retention principles and amplifies breach exposure, remediated by the policy discipline; and the compliance-last-builder whose privacy review arrives after system launch, forcing retrofit costs and residual exposure, remediated by the design integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
