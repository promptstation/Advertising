## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the ethics discipline whose algorithmic-harm patterns the data-analytics field documented first and advertising must not repeat: the bias-mechanism taxonomy (the data-bias channel where historical discrimination, sampling exclusion, and label bias enter through training data whose patterns models faithfully reproduce and amplify per the bias literatures including the Weapons of Math Destruction framework of the sources; the proxy-discrimination mechanics where behavioral and geographic features encode protected attributes through correlation, enabling effectively-discriminatory targeting without explicit sensitive variables per the proxy literatures; the feedback-loop amplification where model outputs influence future data — targeted audiences convert more, generating more training signal — creating self-reinforcing skew per the loop literatures; the objective-mis-specification hazard where optimized metrics diverge from actual business and social value, the engagement-optimization pathology documented across platform literatures); the advertising-specific-harm landscape (the discriminatory-targeting-exclusion history where housing, employment, and credit advertising faced enforcement over exclusionary audience construction, establishing the regulated categories whose targeting demands special handling per the enforcement literatures; the vulnerability-targeting boundary where financial-distress, health-condition, and addiction-adjacent audience exploitation crosses the ethics lines of the behavioral companion per the vulnerability literatures; the manipulation-optimization concern where persuasion models trained toward conversion can discover dark-pattern-adjacent strategies absent explicit constraint per the optimization-ethics literatures; the information-environment effects where micro-targeted messaging at scale raises documented concerns about discourse quality and manipulation opacity per the democracy literatures); the fairness-assessment practice (the fairness-metric literacy where demographic parity, equalized opportunity, and calibration measures capture distinct fairness definitions that mathematically conflict, requiring explicit value choice per the fairness literatures; the disparate-impact-testing discipline where model outcomes face subgroup performance analysis before deployment per the audit traditions; the documentation-and-transparency standards where model cards, data sheets, and limitation statements make bias potential reviewable per the transparency literatures; the mitigation-toolkit literacy where data rebalancing, constraint-based training, post-processing adjustment, and targeting-policy rules address identified harms with the accuracy-equity tradeoff honesty per the mitigation literatures); the responsible-AI governance (the model-risk-management framework where deployment tiering by decision impact determines review depth, validation requirements, and monitoring intensity per the risk literatures; the human-oversight-architecture where consequential automated decisions retain human review channels per the oversight traditions including the automated-decision provisions of privacy regulation; the explainability-obligation management where regulatory and ethical requirements for meaningful explanation meet model complexity per the explainability literatures; the AI-use-policy development where organizational standards govern generative-AI deployment in creative, analysis, and customer interaction per the emerging-policy traditions); the privacy-ethics integration (the data-minimization principle where collection and retention limit to demonstrated need, reducing both harm surface and compliance burden per the privacy frameworks of the quality section; the secondary-use discipline where analytics purposes stay within collection consent boundaries per the purpose-limitation traditions; the re-identification-risk awareness where granular behavioral data permits identity inference despite anonymization, demanding the aggregation and threshold controls of the privacy-engineering section; the consumer-expectation standard where technically-legal practices that violate reasonable expectations accumulate trust damage per the expectation literatures); and the professional-practice culture (the ethics-review-integration where analysis proposals face structured harm assessment per the governance traditions of the behavioral companion; the dissent-and-escalation channels where analysts can flag harmful patterns without career cost per the culture literatures; the industry-standard participation where professional codes, platform policies, and regulatory engagement shape the field's norms per the stewardship traditions; the long-term-trust-accounting where ethical practice compounds into consumer, regulator, and stakeholder trust that extraction strategies destroy per the trust-economics of the quality section) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the bias-blind-deployer whose targeting and scoring models face no subgroup audit until enforcement or press surfaces disparate impact, remediated by the assessment practice; the proxy-launderer whose sensitive-category exclusion runs through correlated behavioral features, remediated by the proxy-discrimination literacy; the feedback-loop-ignorer whose self-reinforcing models concentrate opportunity and scrutiny unevenly without monitoring, remediated by the loop awareness; the metric-monoculture optimizer whose engagement objective overrides declared business and consumer value, remediated by the mis-specification literacy; the explainability-dodger whose black-box deployment meets regulatory explanation requirements with vendor mystique, remediated by the governance framework; and the minimization-scoffer whose collect-everything posture treats privacy law as obstacle rather than harm-reduction architecture, remediated by the privacy-ethics integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
