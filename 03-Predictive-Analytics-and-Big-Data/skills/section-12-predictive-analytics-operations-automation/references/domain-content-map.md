## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Operate analytics as a system through reporting architecture (dashboard hierarchy, metric trees, alerting design, self-service boundaries), automation engineering (report pipelines.
2. Model operations, workflow judgment, AI tooling integration), product thinking (decision-centric design, user research, adoption tracking, literacy enablement), knowledge management (documentation standards, insight repositories, request triage, methodology standardization), quality operations (analytics QA, incident response, dependency management, technical debt), and capability development (skill portfolios.
3. Review culture, tooling currency), avoiding dashboard graveyards, manual slavery, alert fatigue, undocumented heroism, AI-output shipping, and QA skipping failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the operationalization discipline where individual analyses mature into automated, monitored, decision-serving systems: the reporting-architecture design (the dashboard-hierarchy principle where executive summary, operational monitoring, and analyst exploration layers serve different audiences with different refresh and depth requirements per the BI literatures of the infrastructure section; the metric-tree construction where north-star outcomes decompose into driver metrics whose causal logic connects to the attribution and forecasting frameworks per the metrics-architecture traditions; the alerting-design discipline where threshold and anomaly alerts route to owners with escalation paths, avoiding both silence and fatigue per the monitoring traditions of the quality section; the self-service-boundary judgment where governed semantic layers enable business-user exploration without definition drift per the governance frameworks of the quality section); the automation-engineering practice (the report-automation pipeline where scheduled extraction, transformation, and publication replace manual reporting with the exception-handling and notification discipline documented per the automation traditions; the model-operations integration where scoring pipelines, drift monitors, and retraining schedules run the predictive models of the earlier sections in production per the MLOps literatures; the workflow-automation judgment where repetitive analyst tasks including data pulls, standard cuts, and formatting automate while interpretation and recommendation remain human per the hybrid-workflow traditions; the AI-tooling-integration literacy where large-language-model assistants accelerate SQL generation, code debugging, and documentation with the verification requirement that generated output faces correctness and security review per the AI-assistance literatures); the analytics-product thinking (the decision-centric-design principle where every dashboard, model, and report specifies the decision it serves and the action it triggers, the filter that eliminates shelf-ware per the analytics-product traditions; the user-research-application where analyst teams study stakeholder workflows, questions, and friction like any product team per the research traditions; the iteration-and-adoption-tracking where usage analytics on analytics reveal adopted versus abandoned assets guiding investment per the adoption literatures; the data-literacy-enablement practice where stakeholder training on metric definitions, uncertainty, and self-service tools raises organizational consumption quality per the enablement traditions); the knowledge-and-process management (the analysis-documentation standards where question, data, method, assumptions, findings, and limitations persist with every deliverable per the reproducibility traditions of the infrastructure section; the insight-repository systems where validated findings accumulate searchable, preventing re-analysis and building institutional pattern libraries per the knowledge traditions; the request-triage-process where incoming analysis demands face prioritization against decision impact and effort per the operations traditions; the methodology-standardization practice where common analyses including campaign readouts, segmentation refreshes, and forecast cycles follow templates whose deviation requires justification per the standardization traditions); the quality-and-reliability operations (the analytics-QA discipline where deliverables face pre-publication verification of numbers, definitions, and logic per the QA traditions; the incident-response practice where wrong-number publications follow correction, root-cause, and prevention protocols because analytic trust erodes faster than it builds per the incident traditions; the dependency-management awareness where report and model dependencies on upstream pipelines receive failure monitoring per the orchestration literatures of the infrastructure section; the technical-debt-management where expedient scripts and one-off queries accumulate documented obligations per the engineering traditions); and the team-capability development (the skill-portfolio-management where teams balance statistical, engineering, domain, and communication capability per the capability traditions; the mentorship-and-review culture where senior review of analysis and code develops junior rigor per the craft traditions; the tooling-currency practice where platform, language, and method evolution receives managed adoption per the frontier literatures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the dashboard-graveyard keeper whose dozens of unowned reports accumulate views approaching zero because decision-centric design never filtered them, remediated by the product-thinking principle; the manual-report-slave whose analyst weeks vanish into copy-paste cycles that automation engineering solved years ago, remediated by the automation practice; the alert-fatigue-creator whose hundred daily notifications train stakeholders to ignore all of them, remediated by the alerting discipline; the undocumented-hero analyst whose brilliant ad-hoc work dies with their departure because documentation standards never applied, remediated by the knowledge management; the AI-output-shipper whose generated SQL and code reach stakeholders unverified, publishing confident errors at scale, remediated by the verification requirement; and the QA-skipper whose typo-level numeric error in an executive deck destroys months of analytic credibility, remediated by the reliability operations with detection methods as the diagnostic.
