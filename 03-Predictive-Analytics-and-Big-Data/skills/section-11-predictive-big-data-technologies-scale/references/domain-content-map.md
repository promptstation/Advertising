## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the scale-technology literacy whose understanding determines when big-data infrastructure earns its complexity and cost versus when it constitutes engineering theater: the scale-problem definition (the volume-velocity-variety framework where dataset size, generation speed, and structural heterogeneity define the classic big-data dimensions with the veracity and value additions that practitioners emphasize per the big-data literatures; the genuine-scale-triggers where clickstream billions, cross-platform event fusion, real-time personalization, and multi-year impression logs exceed single-machine processing, justifying distributed infrastructure per the architecture traditions; the false-scale-diagnosis where millions of well-structured rows process comfortably in cloud warehouses and Python environments, making premature distributed-system adoption pure cost per the fitness-for-purpose discipline; the marketing-data-volume reality where the largest datasets — impression-level ad logs, event streams, social firehoses — concentrate in platforms whose API-extraction limits often bind before processing capacity does per the ecosystem literatures of the landscape section); the distributed-processing landscape (the batch-processing paradigm where MapReduce-lineage engines including the Spark family process large historical datasets through distributed transformation with the practitioner literacy of job structure, partitioning, and failure handling per the engine literatures; the stream-processing paradigm where event-flow systems including Kafka-lineage pipelines and stream processors handle real-time ingestion and computation for live personalization and monitoring per the streaming literatures; the cloud-warehouse-and-lakehouse convergence where managed platforms deliver SQL-scale analytics over lake storage, simplifying the historical warehouse-lake dichotomy per the platform literatures; the serverless-and-managed trend where cloud services abstract infrastructure operations, shifting skill requirements from cluster administration toward data engineering and cost management per the cloud traditions); the storage-and-format literacy (the columnar-format advantage where analytical query performance derives from storage structures including Parquet-lineage formats whose compression and pruning benefits analysts should understand per the format literatures; the data-lake-organization patterns where directory partitioning, table formats, and catalog metadata determine whether lakes remain queryable or decay into swamps per the organization traditions; the retention-and-tiering economics where hot, warm, and cold storage tiers match access patterns to cost per the lifecycle literatures of the infrastructure section); the real-time-and-activation systems (the real-time-personalization architecture where user events trigger scoring and content decisions within milliseconds through feature stores and inference services per the personalization literatures; the feature-store concept where consistent feature computation serves training and inference preventing the train-serve skew that corrupts production models per the feature-store traditions; the audience-activation pipelines where computed segments and scores sync into ad platforms, CRM systems, and web properties with the frequency, size-threshold, and privacy constraints documented per the activation literatures of the media companion); the engineering-collaboration practice (the analyst-engineer-boundary literacy where analysts specify requirements, validate outputs, and prototype while data engineers productionize, the collaboration whose vocabulary gaps the workflow practices bridge per the collaboration traditions; the pipeline-review participation where analysts who understand transformation logic catch definition errors that pure engineering review misses per the review traditions; the cost-governance participation where query patterns, storage growth, and job frequency face budget monitoring per the cloud-economics discipline of the infrastructure section); and the scale-decision framework (the build-versus-buy judgment where managed platforms, vendor solutions, and self-built infrastructure face evaluation against team capability and strategic importance per the sourcing traditions; the incremental-scaling pathway where warehouse-first architectures extend to distributed processing only when demonstrated need appears per the evolution traditions; the capability-honesty assessment where organizational skill in distributed systems determines whether big-data investment produces analysis or maintenance burden per the capability traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the resume-driven-architecture builder whose Hadoop-style clusters serve datasets a warehouse handles trivially, converting analytics budget into operations burden, remediated by the false-scale diagnosis; the swamp-curator whose unpartitioned, uncataloged lake accumulates files nobody can query or trust, remediated by the organization patterns; the realtime-wannabe whose batch-sufficient use case absorbs streaming complexity and its failure modes, remediated by the scale-trigger discipline; the train-serve-skew sufferer whose prototype features diverge from production computation, corrupting model behavior silently, remediated by the feature-store concept; the API-bound-overbuilder whose processing power idles because platform extraction limits bind first, remediated by the volume-reality literacy; and the capability-overestimating adopter whose team lacks distributed-systems skills to operate what strategy purchased, remediated by the honesty assessment with detection methods as the diagnostic.
