## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the identity-structure discipline where the major cultural categories through which people locate themselves receive anthropological treatment rather than marketing stereotype: the identity-category framework (the social-identity-theory connection where group membership constitutes self-concept with belonging, distinction, and esteem dynamics per the identity literatures of the subculture section; the intersectionality principle where categories combine into positioned experience that no single-category analysis captures per the intersection traditions; the constructed-versus-lived distinction where analytical categories differ from experienced identity, the gap whose collapse produces the essentialism failures of the foundations section; the fluidity-and-change awareness where category meanings and boundaries shift historically, making static treatment analytically false per the change literatures); the gender-and-identity-system (the gender-as-cultural-system understanding where masculinity and femininity operate as learned symbolic systems varying across cultures and eras per the gender literatures; the representation-history literacy where advertising's gendered imagery carries documented patterns of stereotype whose critique reshaped industry standards per the representation traditions of the Hall framework in the sources; the contemporary-gender-marketing dynamics where inclusive representation, identity-affirming product design, and stereotype avoidance coexist with backlash politics per the current literatures; the category-specific-sensitivity where gendered marketing in toys, beauty, fashion, and personal care faces distinct norms and scrutiny per the category literatures); the generation-analysis discipline (the cohort-effect-literacy where genuinely shared historical experience — economic conditions, technology adoption windows, cultural events — creates real cohort patterns per the cohort literatures; the generation-horoscope-critique where named-generation personality claims conflate life-stage effects, period effects, and cohort effects whose separation the analytics traditions demand per the critique literatures of the course pitfalls; the life-stage-versus-cohort distinction where twenty-five-year-old behavior tracks life stage more than birth cohort for most consumption, the confusion that misdirects campaigns per the distinction literatures; the intergenerational-dynamics analysis where family influence flows, technology mediation, and value transmission structure cross-generational marketing per the dynamics literatures); the class-and-taste-system (the Bourdieu-framework application where economic capital, cultural capital, and social capital combine into habitus — durable dispositions shaping taste and consumption per the distinction literatures of the sources; the taste-as-class-marker analysis where aesthetic preference, leisure practice, and consumption style function as social positioning per the taste traditions; the aspirational-and-distancing-dynamics where consumption signals class trajectory in both directions per the mobility literatures; the contemporary-class-complexity where credential expansion, platform culture, and economic precarity reshape traditional class-consumption mapping per the complexity literatures); the ethnicity-race-and-representation system (the ethnicity-as-meaning-system understanding where cultural heritage, community practice, and identity experience shape consumption beyond skin-deep targeting per the ethnicity literatures; the representation-and-authenticity requirements where casting, storytelling, cultural detail, and creative-team composition determine whether representation reads as genuine per the authenticity literatures of the later section; the stereotype-mechanism-analysis where media representation patterns carry documented harm whose avoidance requires structural understanding rather than checklist compliance per the stereotype literatures of the Hall traditions; the multicultural-market-dynamics where ethnic-community media, retail, influencers, and institutions constitute distinct market infrastructure per the market literatures); the applied-identity-strategy practice (the audience-identity-mapping methodology where target populations get analyzed across operating identity categories with the ethnographic evidence standards of the methods section per the mapping traditions; the representation-review-discipline where campaign assets face systematic review for stereotype, exclusion, and authenticity per the review traditions of the practice section; the community-consultation-practice where identity-relevant campaigns engage community voices in development per the consultation traditions connecting to the ethics section; the category-relevance-judgment where identity dimensions receive attention proportionate to actual relevance rather than token inclusion per the judgment literatures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the single-category-reducer whose gender, generation, or ethnicity campaign addresses one dimension of intersectional identity, missing the actual audience, remediated by the intersectionality principle; the generation-horoscope-planner whose Gen-Z strategy lists birth-year personality traits instead of analyzing cohort conditions and life stage, remediated by the critique discipline; the class-blind-positioner whose taste assumptions reflect the creative team's cultural capital while targeting audiences whose habitus differs, remediated by the Bourdieu framework; the token-representation-includer whose diverse casting fronts homogeneous creative teams and stereotyped narratives, remediated by the authenticity requirements; the stereotype-checklist-complier whose review ticks boxes without structural understanding of representational harm, remediated by the mechanism analysis; and the identity-irrelevance-assumer whose category judgment ignores that identity dimensions carry commercial weight in their market, remediated by the relevance-judgment practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
